## Engle–Granger two-step

1. **OLS hedge:** `y = α + β x + u`
2. **ADF on residual u.** The null is “no cointegration.” Important: **residual-based critical values differ from a plain ADF** on a raw series — use **MacKinnon** tables (what `statsmodels.tsa.stattools.coint` reports). Applying `adfuller` p-values directly to EG residuals is **anti-conservative**.

Lab also computes a **hand ADF t-stat** on Δu = a + φ u_(t-1) + γ Δu_(t-1) + e and compares φ’s t to `coint` / `adfuller`.

### Full-sample results (key numbers)

| pair | β | coint p (MacKinnon) | plain ADF p on resid | hand t_φ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Y_coin/BBB | 1.215 | **0.00052** | 0.00026 | −4.44 |
| Y_drift/BBB | 1.363 | **0.972** | 0.914 | −0.38 |
| AAA/BBB | 0.123 | 0.829 | 0.633 | −1.29 |

Y_coin rejects; Y_drift and AAA fail — as designed.

## Rolling cointegration pitfall

Window=80 rolling `coint` p-values: Y_coin median p≈**0.16**, frac(p&lt;0.05)≈**0.36**; Y_drift median≈**0.66**, frac≈**0.01**. A single full-sample pass can hide windows that flip in/out of cointegration — **never check EG only once in-sample**.

## Optional: Kalman dynamic hedge ratio (filter vs smoother)

**Intuition:** the hedge ratio β may drift slowly. Rolling OLS estimates β on a fixed window; a Kalman filter treats β as a hidden state that “only wanders a little each day” and updates its estimate every time a new data point arrives.

**Model:** state equation `β_t = β_(t−1) + w_t`, observation equation `y_t = β_t · x_t + v_t` (`w_t, v_t` are noise with variances Q, R).

**The key filter vs smoother difference (per the [statsmodels state-space docs](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/statespace.html)):** the docs state that “filtered” output at time t is an estimate conditional on **observations up through time t** (one-sided); “smoothed” output is conditional on **the entire set of observations in the dataset** (two-sided). So a **smoothed β uses future data** — fine for after-the-fact analysis, never for generating that day’s trading signal.

**Numeric example (`examples/p3/05_kalman_hedge.py`, seed=5, synthetic; true β jumps from 1.0 to 1.5 at t=150):**

```text
t=149: true=1.00 filtered=1.0090 smoothed=1.2098
mean |beta error| filtered=0.0137 smoothed=0.0122
max |filtered change| for t<=t0: 0.000000
max |smoothed change| for t<=t0: 0.345708
```

How to read it: at t=149 the jump has not happened yet, yet the smoothed β has already moved “early” to 1.21 — it saw the future. The smoothed error is smaller only because it peeked at the answer. **Future-perturbation test:** edit the data after t0=200; filtered values for t≤t0 do not change at all (0.000000), while smoothed values do (0.3457). A causal signal must pass this test.

**Practice:** build the spread with the one-period-lagged filtered β (`β_(t−1)`); the choice of Q and R is itself a parameter and counts toward your trial count.

disclaimer: synthetic data; learning only, not investment advice.

## Shorting US stocks is not just a “negative weight” (checked 2026-11-08)

Reg SHO layers ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)): Rule 200 marking (long/short); Rule 203(b) locate; Rule 201 price test (after a ≥10% one-day drop, for the rest of that day and the next); Rule 204 close-out of fails. Plus: borrow fees, availability, recall/buy-in risk; the short seller **pays dividends** to the lender; losses are **unlimited** in theory. (The SEC page’s T+2 passages are outdated; settlement is now T+1.)

**Lab:** `examples/p3/06_us_short_constraints.py` (synthetic data):

```text
cumulative: long_only -0.0338, naive_pair -0.0749, constrained_pair -0.0559
short leg rejected or flat on 25 of 60 days; borrow fee 0.00556; dividend owed 0.00400
```

The numbers only show that constraints change results; they are **not** a verdict on the strategy.

**A locate is not a completed borrow (checked 2026-11-11).** Rule 203(b)(1) says a broker-dealer may not accept or effect a short sale unless it has borrowed the security, entered into a bona-fide arrangement to borrow it, or has reasonable grounds to believe the security can be borrowed so that it can be delivered on the date delivery is due, and has documented compliance ([17 CFR 242.203(b)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.203)). So a locate can be only "reasonable grounds that the shares can be borrowed by settlement"; it is **not** the same as already having borrowed the stock. Undocumented grounds do not complete the locate. In a backtest, do not write "the short was allowed" as if the borrow were locked in and recall-proof.

**Read Rule 204's close-out clock from the current T+1 rule text.** If a participant of a registered clearing agency has a fail to deliver in an equity security, it must generally close out that fail, by buying or borrowing like kind and quantity, no later than the **beginning of regular trading hours on the settlement day following the settlement date** ([17 CFR 242.204(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.204); amended for T+1 at 89 FR 26608, 2024-04-15). Under T+1, a short-sale fail is therefore usually due by the open two settlement days after the trade (in the lab: a Monday 2026-11-23 trade settles 11-24 and must be closed out by the 11-25 open). If the participant can show the fail came from a long sale, or from bona-fide market making, the deadline is the beginning of regular hours on the **third** consecutive settlement day following the settlement date. Some older SEC FAQ pages still label these clocks T+4 and T+6; those names date from T+3 settlement and should not be copied. Settlement holidays such as Thanksgiving push the dates forward.

**The Rule 201 price test is "not at a price less than or equal to the current national best bid."** After a covered security falls 10% or more from the listing market's prior-day close, a trading center's policies must prevent the execution or display of a short sale at a price that is **less than or equal to the current national best bid (NBB)**, for the rest of that day and the next ([17 CFR 242.201(b)(1)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.201)). A short sale one tick above the NBB is not barred by this test.

**Threshold securities have a statutory definition.** Under Rule 203(c)(6), an equity security of a reporting issuer is a threshold security if the aggregate fail to deliver position at a registered clearing agency is **10,000 shares or more and at least 0.5% of shares outstanding** for five consecutive settlement days, and the security is on a list an SRO disseminates to its members ([17 CFR 242.203(c)(6)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.203)). Both size tests must be met. The general close-out clock is Rule 204, which is tighter than the older 13-settlement-day description.

## Short-side public data: three datasets, three meanings (checked 2026-11-10)

If you add short-side data to a pairs or factor study, first know what each public file measures and **when it became public**.

| Dataset | What it is | When it is public | Common wrong reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| FINRA short interest | A **stock**: open short positions member firms report under FINRA Rule 4560, **twice a month** (settlement dates around the 15th and month-end) | Firms report by 6 p.m. ET on the 2nd business day; FINRA publishes on about the **7th business day** after the settlement date (see FINRA's yearly calendar) | Joining it on the settlement date |
| FINRA daily short sale volume | A **flow**: short-sale trades reported to FINRA's TRF/ADF/ORF (off-exchange) during normal hours | Daily | Calling it "short interest" or "the market's short ratio" |
| SEC fails-to-deliver (FTD) | An outstanding **balance** of fails at NSCC's CNS system on each settlement date | First half of a month around month-end; second half around the 15th of the next month | Reading it as daily new fails or proof of naked shorting |

- **Point-in-time rule:** in FINRA's 2026 calendar, the 2026-10-15 snapshot is published on 2026-10-26. A signal on 2026-10-23 may use only the 2026-09-30 snapshot (published 2026-10-09). Joining by settlement date is look-ahead.
- **Short volume is not short interest.** FINRA's Information Notice of 2019-05-10 says the daily file "does not—and is not intended to—equate to short interest". Its example: off-exchange data alone shows a 20% short ratio; adding the exchange volume gives 10.7% for the same day.
- **FTD:** the SEC says the figure "is not a daily amount of fails" and that fails are "not evidence of abusive short selling or 'naked' short selling".
- **Proposal is not rule:** FINRA proposed weekly short-interest reporting in SR-FINRA-2026-012 (May 2026). The SEC page marks it **withdrawn on 2026-08-05**, so twice-monthly reporting still applies.
- **Not public yet: SEC Form SHO and securities-lending data.** Rule 13f-2 (adopted 2023-10-13) requires institutional investment managers above set thresholds to report short positions on Form SHO within 14 calendar days after each month end, and the SEC is to publish aggregated data per security. Rule 10c-1a requires securities loans to be reported to a registered national securities association (FINRA), which is to publish certain of the data. On 2025-08-25 the Fifth Circuit remanded both rules to the SEC, without vacatur, to consider their cumulative economic impact. On 2025-12-03 the SEC extended the exemptions: Form SHO compliance until **2028-01-02** (the first reports, for January 2028, are due 14 calendar days after that month ends), 10c-1a reporting until **2028-09-28** and its public dissemination until **2029-03-29** ([SEC 34-104303](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2025/34-104303.pdf)). So no backtest period up to now can contain official Form SHO aggregates, and a 2026 series sold under that name is something else; check its source. The SEC may still amend the rules before then. The 2026 regulatory agenda ([RIN 3235-AN72](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?RIN=3235-AN72&pubId=202510)) plans to amend 13f-2 / Form SHO and 10c-1a; as of 2026-11-11 no proposal had been published.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Trading high corr without EG / residual stationarity.
2. Using plain ADF critical values on EG residuals.
3. Checking cointegration only once on the full sample.
4. Costing one leg only (lab forces n_legs=2).
5. Estimating β including the test window.
6. Generating historical signals from the Kalman **smoothed** β (look-ahead leak); or using the same-period filtered β without a one-period lag.
7. A backtest where the short leg is always borrowable, fee-free and pays no dividends.
8. Treating SEC Form SHO aggregates as data that already exist (compliance is exempted until 2028-01-02); joining short interest on its settlement date instead of its publish date; calling FINRA daily short volume "short interest"; reading an FTD balance as new daily fails or proof of naked shorting.
9. Treating a locate as a completed borrow; using the T+3-era FAQ labels “T+4 / T+6” as the current close-out clock; or reading Rule 201 as “no short selling after a 10% drop.”
