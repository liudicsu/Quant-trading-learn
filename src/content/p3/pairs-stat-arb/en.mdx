## Learning goals

This lesson (~**10 hours**: 4h concepts + 5h lab + 1h write-up) builds a **pairs** residual, compares **static vs rolling hedge ratios**, and separates **correlation from cointegration intuition**. Prereq: `mean-reversion`.

**Goals:** rolling-beta spread; discuss broken legs.

**Must-teach:** high correlation is not enough; cointegration intuition; re-estimate hedge ratios.

## Correlation ≠ tradeable

Lab: return correlation of the cointegrated foil ≈ **0.98**, and a **drifting intercept foil** can keep similarly high correlation while residual std blows up (**0.73 → 4.50**). You need an approximately **stationary residual**, not just high corr.

## Static vs rolling hedge

Static β≈**1.22**; rolling mean β≈**1.23**. Net of two-leg costs: static net SR≈**−0.48** (NAV≈0.96); rolling net SR≈**0.19** (NAV≈1.01). OOS still harsh (rolling OOS≈**−0.45**) — report it.

![Pairs NAV](/p3/pairs_nav.png)

## Broken-leg / shock note

If one leg gaps on idiosyncratic news, a frozen hedge leaves residual beta; rolling adapts with lag=ROLL. Expect a burst of wideners / stop-outs before β re-learns. Lab: mean |β_roll−β_static| over last 60 bars ≈ **0.07** on this foil (larger after a true break).

## Look-ahead warnings

- OLS window at t uses only past bars `[t−w, t)`.
- β used in today’s PnL must be lagged (`shift(1)`).
- Do not pick the pair because the full-sample residual “looked stationary” after you already saw the test window.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Trading high corr without checking residual stability.
2. Estimating β on the full sample including test.
3. Costing only one leg.
4. Ignoring structural breaks / corporate actions on a leg.
5. Reading quants.md headline Return% as an edge certificate.
