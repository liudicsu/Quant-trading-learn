## TSMOM vs cross-sectional (classic papers)

**Cross-sectional (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993, *JF*):** rank names by past J-month returns; long winners / short losers for K months. Relative strength across peers. Skip-a-week variants reduce bid-ask / short-term reversal noise; industry **12-1** skips the most recent month for the same motive ([PDF](https://bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/jegadeesh-titman93.pdf)).

**Time-series (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012, *JFE* 104(2), pp. 228–250):** each instrument trades on **its own** past excess return (typically past 1–12 months), not on a cross-sectional rank. They document TSMOM across 58 liquid futures (equity index, FX, commodity, bonds); persistence for about a year then **partial reversal**; a diversified TSMOM book shows little exposure to standard factors. In **their 2012 sample**, diversified TSMOM did relatively well in some extreme months — a **historical paper finding**, not a live tip. Prefer the [RePEc/JFE abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v104y2012i2p228-250.html) over firm marketing summaries (e.g. AQR blog notes).

**Lab mapping:** AAA sign-of-past-return = **TSMOM foil**; long-best/short-worst among `AAA, BBB, CCC` = **CS toy** (too few names for institutional CS).

## Expected-value framing (depth)

Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen emphasize predictability from an instrument’s **own** 1–12 month excess return and a partial reversal beyond about a year; Jegadeesh–Titman emphasize **relative** strength across names. The two can coexist: decompositions often find positive auto-covariance as a shared driver, but implementations and risk exposures differ. Our toy panel is far too short to reproduce their cross-asset significance — we borrow definitions and contrasts only, with no live extrapolation.

Turnover and crowding: when more capital chases the same signal, impact and slippage rise and expected net edge erodes. That is why every table here reports turnover and reuses the P2 four-part cost stack instead of gross-only NAVs.

## Skip-window motive

On **this** short panel, no-skip can beat skip in-sample. That does not refute JT/Moskowitz design motives: skip reduces microstructure / short-horizon reversal contamination. Expected-value framing — bias reduction, not a single-sample guarantee.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Treating TSMOM and CS as the same factor.
2. Literal 12-month windows on a 1-year toy without stating scale.
3. Celebrating gross curves before costs.
4. CS on three names as institutional evidence.
5. Same-bar prices in the formation window.
