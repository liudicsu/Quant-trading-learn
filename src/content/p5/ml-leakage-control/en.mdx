## Parameters

- Panel: P4 weak, **50** assets × 750 days; `HORIZON=5`; `N_FOLDS=5`; `EMBARGO=5`
- Features as of `t`: `mom_*`, `vol_*`, `rank_mom20` from returns ≤ `t`
- **Triple-barrier (AFML ch.3):** `pt = sl = k·σ_t` with `k=1`, `σ_t` = **lagged EWMA** vol (`span=20`, `shift(1)`) — **not** fixed 2%
- **Primary metric:** mean over days of cross-sectional Spearman RankIC; report **t-stat** and **ICIR**. Pooled Spearman is only a **counterexample** (mixes time-series and cross-sectional variation).

## Why fixed 2% bands are wrong

Assets and regimes differ in volatility. A flat ±2% barrier is hit constantly by noisy high-vol names and almost never by quiet low-vol names, so the label distribution becomes **vol-driven** rather than information-driven. AFML scales horizontal barriers by a causal volatility estimate so each name/time gets a comparable hurdle. Lab `tb_pt` spans ~0.7%–4.1% across the panel (median ≈1.9%).

## Labels and look-ahead

- `fwd_ret[t] = sum(rets[t+1..t+h])`
- Vol-scaled TB walks the path from `t+1`; `σ_t` uses only past returns
- Meta-label: trade iff `sign(mom_20[t])` agrees with barrier side
- Purge removes train rows whose label span overlaps the test block (+ embargo)

## Primary metric

**Mean daily CS RankIC** = average_t Spearman(pred_t, y_t) within the cross-section on day t.

**Pooled Spearman** stacks every (date, asset) row into one correlation — it mixes serial dependence with cross-sectional ranking and can even **disagree in sign** with the daily CS mean (lab: purged ridge pooled ≈ −0.008 while mean daily CS IC ≈ **+0.040**).

## Why shuffle may not inflate IC here

On a **panel**, row-shuffle mixes same-day names across folds. With a **weak** cross-sectional signal, that scrambling does not reliably raise mean daily CS RankIC — here shuffle ≈+0.035 sits **below** purged ridge ≈+0.040 because noise dominates. Use clearer leaks: (i) full-sample standardization (a real leak class, but **subtle** — on this sample it did **not** inflate vs purged) and especially (ii) a feature that is a noisy copy of the forward label (lab IC ≈+0.93 ≫ oracle ≈+0.10).

## Three cases (do not collapse them)

1. **(a) Pipeline illusion** — clear **label** leaks explode IC (noisy fwd ≈+0.93 ≫ oracle); unpurged CV mildly inflates (≈0.052 > purged ≈0.040). Full-sample standardization is a real leak class but was **subtle here** (IC ≈ purged ≈+0.040 — did not inflate on this sample). Panel shuffle alone may fail to inflate a weak CS signal.
2. **(b) Weak but recoverable** — after purge+embargo, ridge/GBM still recover ~**+0.04** mean CS IC on this planted weak panel (oracle ~0.10 is the ceiling).
3. **(c) Real IC, costs wipe net SR** — lab 02: purged ridge IC ≈+0.04 yet **net SR ≈ −0.38** under the shared cost stack (high turnover). Positive IC ≠ tradeable edge.

A purged IC near zero would mean “no recoverable linear signal under this protocol” — **not** the universal “correct answer.” On this panel the purged answer is a **small positive** IC.

## Fractional differentiation (AFML ch.5)

**Intuition:** Prices are usually non-stationary. Taking an integer difference \(d=1\) (simple returns) often passes a stationarity test **but wipes long memory** — the very dependence a slow signal might need. **Fractional differentiation** applies fractional weights


```text
\omega_0=1,\quad \omega_k=-\omega_{k-1}\frac{d-k+1}{k}
```


and cuts the sum when \(|\omega_k|\) falls under a threshold (fixed-width window). You search for the smallest \(d\) that looks stationary while keeping more serial dependence than \(d=1\).

**Numeric toy** (`examples/p5/03_afml_depth.py`, synthetic log-price):

- ADF on raw price: p≈**0.15** (fail to reject unit root)
- Fracdiff \(d=0.4\): ADF p≈**0.00**; autocorr(lag=20)≈**+0.17**
- Integer diff \(d=1\): ADF p≈**0.00**; autocorr(lag=20)≈**−0.02**

So both transforms can be “stationary,” but fracdiff **keeps memory** the integer difference destroys. When \(d\) is a positive integer, weights beyond lag \(d\) are exactly zero — memory cancelled by construction.

## Sample uniqueness & sequential bootstrap (AFML ch.4)

**Intuition:** Triple-barrier (and many horizon) labels **overlap in time**. Counting each overlapping event as an IID sample overstates the effective sample size. **Average uniqueness** of an event is the mean of \(1/c_t\) over the bars \(t\) it spans, where \(c_t\) is the number of concurrent labels at \(t\).

**Tiny panel:** 12 overlapping events. Concurrent counts rise to 4 in the middle. Equal-weight mean of \(y\) ≈ **0.0075**; uniqueness-weighted mean ≈ **0.0094** — redundant concurrent labels are down-weighted.

**Sequential bootstrap (one paragraph):** ordinary bagging draws IDs with equal probability, so overlapping events keep co-appearing. Sequential bootstrap (AFML ch.4) draws the next index with probability proportional to **remaining** uniqueness given events already drawn, producing less redundant bags.

## Meta-labeling filter (AFML ch.3) — full subsection

**Primary side** decides direction (here `sign(mom_20)`). **Meta-labeling** learns a second model whose label is “trade or skip” (agree with the barrier outcome). It does **not** invent a new side; it filters when to size the primary bet. Bet sizing after the filter continues in `modern-ml-pipelines` (AFML ch.10).

| Config | mean daily CS RankIC | turnover | net SR | trade_frac |
|--------|----------------------|----------|--------|------------|
| A primary always | ≈+0.032 | ≈0.16 | ≈+0.17 | 1.00 |
| B meta filter p≥0.52 | ≈+0.035 | ≈0.31 | ≈−1.46 | ≈0.36 |
| C meta filter p≥0.48 | ≈+0.030 | ≈0.23 | ≈+0.10 | ≈0.95 |

On this weak panel, a stricter meta filter can **raise IC a little** while **hurting net SR** via turnover — another (b)/(c) reminder: filtering ≠ free edge after costs.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Fixed % barriers that encode volatility, not information.
2. Using pooled Spearman as the headline IC.
3. Declaring “purged IC≈0 is always correct” when a weak signal is recoverable.
4. Shuffling rows / unpurged KFold / global scalers.
5. Celebrating train IC from noisy deep trees.
6. Using integer differencing and calling the series “feature-ready” without checking memory loss.
7. Treating overlapping labels as IID (skip uniqueness weights).
8. Confusing meta-label filters with a new directional alpha.
