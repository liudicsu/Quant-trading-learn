## Parameters

- Liquidation: `X0 = 1e6` shares over `T = 1` day, `N = 10` slices; daily `σ = 0.02`; temporary `η = 1e-6`; permanent `γ = 1e-7`; `S0 = 100`
- AC: `κ ≈ √(λ σ² / η)`; remaining shares `x(t) = X0 · sinh(κ(T−t)) / sinh(κT)`
- λ sweep: `logspace(-8, -3, 12)` → frontier `(E[cost], risk std)`
- Schedules: TWAP equal slices; VWAP matches a U-shaped expected volume profile
- Limit toy: half-spread 0.02, target edge 0.05, fill_p ≈ 0.55, 2000 sims

## TWAP and VWAP

**TWAP** spreads quantity evenly in time — ignores the volume profile. **VWAP** matches expected volume (here a U-shape: heavier open/close). Lab VWAP slices peak ≈ 1.61e5 at the wings vs TWAP 1.00e5 flat. Schedules use **ex-ante** volume forecasts only.

## Almgren & Chriss (2000/2001)

Almgren & Chriss (*Journal of Risk* 3(2), pp. 5–39; Dec 2000 working paper / 2001 issue) minimise expected cost plus a risk penalty from price volatility while shares remain. With linear temporary/permanent impact the **efficient frontier** of cost vs risk is explicit; higher risk-aversion λ → larger κ → faster (“urgent”) liquidation.

Lab λ sweep moves from patient (low E premium, higher inventory risk) to urgent (higher expected impact cost, lower risk). Trajectories for patient / balanced / urgent λ are plotted.

## Implementation shortfall (Perold 1988)

Perold (*Journal of Portfolio Management* 14(3), pp. 4–9) defines **implementation shortfall** as paper portfolio (decision / arrival price) minus realised execution. Lab sell programme (after fixing permanent impact to the **incremental** form `mid += dW − γ·n_j`): arrival 100, paper 1e8, realised ≈ 9.90e7, **IS ≈ 1.038e6 (~103.8 bps)** — matches the reviewer ~104 bps. Decompose delay vs trading (impact) under the arrival-price benchmark.

## Limit vs market (toy)

Market orders pay the half-spread for immediacy (lab mean cost 0.02). Limits improve price *conditional on fill* but add non-fill risk and **adverse selection** when fills cluster on moves against you. Lab: fill rate ≈ 0.56, limit mean PnL ≈ −0.028 under a simple adverse foil — limits are not free edge.

## Slippage monitoring / TCA mini-report

**Identity:** the *volume-weighted* arrival-price slippage equals Perold IS in bps — `Σ (arrival − exec)·n / (arrival·X0)`. Always report that quantity as IS.

On this path slice sizes are nearly equal, so the unweighted mean of per-slice arrival slips **coincides** with the volume-weighted Perold number (~103.8 bps). They diverge only when slice sizes differ — do not treat “unweighted ≠ Perold” as a universal claim.

**Interval VWAP** here uses the **planned** VWAP schedule weights (`vol_profile`), not realized tape volume. Lab volume-weighted benchmarks: **arrival/Perold ≈ 103.8 bps**, **planned-weight interval VWAP ≈ 100.0 bps**, **close ≈ 95.4 bps**. Export `examples/p6/data/tca_report.csv`.

## Look-ahead warnings

- Schedules and AC trajectories use only ex-ante σ, volume, and impact params
- Post-trade TCA may use the realised path — never feed realised fills back into the *decision* schedule without a clear sim protocol
- Impact parameters fitted on the same path you evaluate are circular

## Square-root / double-√ impact literacy

[arXiv:2502.16246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16246) (*The “double” square-root law*, TSE ID-resolved data) and companion [arXiv:2411.13965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13965) (strict √ universality on TSE) teach **mechanical** impact literacy:

- Child orders already obey √ in volume *after digestion*; mesoscopic metaorder √ emerges from a **double** square-root: √ volume × inverse-√ time decay.
- **Scramble foil:** reshuffling trader IDs yields synthetic metaorders with the **same** √Q law → impact looks mechanical, not “informed metaorder detection.”
- Schedule caveat: average impact is often roughly **independent of total time T** at moderate participation — not a free speed dial.
- Tension with **Almgren–Chriss linear η**: AC remains a desk **cost–risk utility** frontier; empirics favour concave √. Keep both: AC for trajectories, √ as the **capacity cost ruler**.

**Formula (cost ruler, not edge):** `I ≈ Y·σ·√(Q/V)`. **Reject** optimising Y on the evaluation path as “alpha.”

Lab toy (`Y=1`, `σ=0.02`, `V=1e6`):

| Q/V | √ impact (bps) | linear foil (bps) | √ / lin |
|-----|----------------|-------------------|---------|
| 0.001 | 6.32 | 0.20 | 31.6 |
| 0.01 | 20.00 | 2.00 | 10.0 |
| 0.05 | 44.72 | 10.00 | 4.47 |
| 0.10 | 63.25 | 20.00 | 3.16 |
| 0.20 | 89.44 | 40.00 | 2.24 |

Double-√ child cumulative (`q=1e4`, `i0=4`): i=1 → 4.72 bps … i=25 → 67.70 bps; plain metaorder √ at `Q=q·25` → 100 bps (N-dependent factor in the double-√ recovery — teaching sketch, not production calibration).

### Gatheral no-dynamic-arbitrage (one paragraph)

[Gatheral (2010), *Quantitative Finance* 10(7), 749–759](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v10y2010i7p749-759.html) (RePEc/DOI verified): no-dynamic-arbitrage links the **shape** of impact to its **decay**. In particular, **exponential decay is compatible only with linear impact** — another reason the AC linear-η + fast decay combo is a modelling tension next to empirical √. Literacy only — not an arb recipe.

<Callout type="warn" title="Debunk">
√ impact is a **cost ruler**. Do not ship “optimize Y → alpha,” Obizhaeva–Wang N-trader games, or RL execution papers as course edge.
</Callout>




## Why public-data metaorder impact is hard (arXiv:2501.17096)

[arXiv:2501.17096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17096) (Naviglio et al.): when you build metaorder generalized IRFs from **public** price–trade models (TIM / Hasbrouck VAR / CNN–LSTM), the paths often disagree with **real metaorder** stylized facts.

| | Real metaorders (literature) | Public-data IRFs (paper's linear/nonlinear fits) |
|--|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| During execution | **Concave** growth (often near √t) | Near-**linear** (sometimes post-end inertia) |
| After the end | **Convex** partial reversion | **Weak** reversion / price may keep drifting |

**LMF literacy:** long memory in order flow ≈ many overlapping metaorders; your new child order does **not** trigger the same same-sign volume the unconditional VAR implies.

**Modified TIM α literacy:** α∈[0,1] = share of child volume that enters the **market volume** equation; α→1 near criticality → linear/inertia; smaller α closer to concave+revert. Lab toy (subcritical):

```
alpha | peak | end | revert_frac | below_chord_during
0.00 | 27.259 | 33.085 | -0.214 | +2.173
0.35 | 48.979 | 89.257 | -0.822 | +8.687
1.00 | 89.315 | 193.578 | -1.167 | +20.785
```

Higher α → weaker post-end reversion / more inertia risk (negative revert). **Protocol/warn: public tape often mis-estimates large metaorder impact.**

<Callout type="warn" title="Debunk">
Do **not** calibrate α on the evaluation path as alpha; no LOB HFT / Hawkes / GAN / MPC execution recipes. This paper is **protocol + warning**, not impact alpha.
</Callout>

## Rule 605 vs FINRA 21-23: public statistics vs broker duty (checked 2026-11-08)

They do not overlap: **Rule 605** is the monthly public execution-quality **statistics** from market centers and larger brokers (calculation in `costs-in-backtest`, [SEC FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/trading-markets-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-rule-605-regulation-nms)); **FINRA 21-23** is about a broker's best-execution **duty** and regular reviews under Rule 5310, which is not only about price ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/21-23)). 605 data can be one input to such a review, but it covers only regular-hours covered orders, so it cannot replace your own arrival-price TCA or rank brokers.

## Rule 606: where your orders went, and what your broker was paid (checked 2026-11-10)

**Routing reports complement execution-quality reports.** Payment for order flow (PFOF) is cash or any other benefit a broker receives for routing customer orders to a particular market maker or exchange ([Rule 10b-10(d)(8)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-240.10b-10)). [Rule 606](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.606) requires every broker to publish a quarterly report on two groups of "non-directed" orders (the customer did not pick the venue): for NMS stocks, those submitted as "held" (the broker must execute them promptly, with no discretion over price or time); for options, customer orders. Month by month, it lists the ten venues that received the most of these orders, plus any other venue that received 5% or more. For each venue, it shows the net amount from PFOF, profit-sharing, transaction fees, and rebates, both in total dollars and per share, broken out across four order types: market, marketable limit, non-marketable limit, and other. S&P 500 stocks, other stocks and options get separate sections. The report is due within one month after the quarter ends and stays online for three years. A customer can also ask the broker for the routing of their own orders over the prior six months. Rule 605 (previous section) covers execution quality; 606 covers routing and money. Read them together. This lesson does not claim PFOF makes fills better or worse. Status: the SEC's 2022 Order Competition Rule and Regulation Best Execution proposals were formally withdrawn on 2025-06-12 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/06/order-competition-rule)); neither is in force.

## Form ATS-N and off-exchange liquidity (checked 2026-11-11)

Rule 606 (previous section) tells you where a broker sent non-directed orders. Some of those destinations are not exchanges. An **alternative trading system** (ATS) can match buyers and sellers under an exemption from exchange registration, if it complies with [Regulation ATS](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/part-242/subject-group-ECFRd043a8fc6fa68e1) ([17 CFR 242.300](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.300)). An ATS that trades NMS stocks must file a public [Form ATS-N](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.304) (Rule 304). The SEC keeps a list of these filings ([Form ATS-N Filings and Information](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets/alternative-trading-systems/form-ats-n-filings-information); page last reviewed 2026-03-30, with later 2026 effective dates in the table, opened 2026-11-11).

Many NMS Stock ATSs do not publicly display their best bids and offers, which is why people call them "dark pools." Their trades still print to the tape, usually as off-exchange or Trade Reporting Facility (TRF) prints. In a backtest this matters in two ways. First, the size you see at the displayed national best bid or offer is not all of the liquidity; some of it sits in ATSs that do not display. Second, a large share of volume may be marked off-exchange, which is not the same as "no venue" or "bad data." Do not invent a fill inside a dark pool from the displayed quote. This lesson does not recommend any ATS or broker.


## Rule 604: a customer limit that improves the quote generally must be displayed (checked 2026-11-12)

The previous sections already taught that Rule 606’s NMS-stock reports cover “held” orders, and that the size you see at the displayed NBBO is not all of the liquidity. [17 CFR 242.604](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.604) is a different duty. For NMS stocks, each exchange specialist (or a member authorized to perform substantially similar functions) and each OTC market maker must “**publish immediately** a bid or offer that reflects” the price and the full size of each customer limit order it holds “that is at a price that would **improve** the bid or offer,” and the full size of a customer limit that is priced **equal** to that specialist’s or OTC market maker’s bid or offer **and** equal to the **NBBO**, if it represents more than a de minimis size change.

Exceptions in 604(b) include: the order is executed upon receipt; the customer “**expressly requests** … that the order **not be displayed**”; an **odd-lot** order (already taught as not protected / not the NBBO); a **block size** order, “unless a customer placing such order requests that the order be displayed”; the order is immediately delivered to another exchange, association-sponsored system, or ECN that complies with the cited display rule, or to another member or OTC market maker that complies with this section; and an “all or none” order. [17 CFR 242.600](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.600) defines **block size** as at least **10,000 shares** or a quantity with market value of at least **$200,000**.

**Hidden is an exception, not the default.** The customer must request non-display, except for the listed exceptions such as odd-lot and block. Do not assume every resting customer limit is in the displayed quote. Do not conflate this display duty with Rule 606’s “held” reporting category, or with Rule 611 trade-through protection. No routing or venue recommendation.

## FINRA 5320: a firm holding your unexecuted customer equity order generally cannot trade ahead of it (checked 2026-11-12)

[FINRA Rule 5320(a)](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5320): a member that “accepts and holds an order in an **equity security** from its own customer or a customer of another broker-dealer **without immediately executing** the order is **prohibited from trading that security on the same side of the market for its own account at a price that would satisfy the customer order**, unless it **immediately thereafter executes the customer order up to the size and at the same or better price** at which it traded for its own account.” The member must have a written, consistently applied methodology consistent with this Rule and Rule 5310 (already taught as best execution, not solely about price).

Exceptions exist in the supplementary material (among them: large or institutional orders with specified disclosure and the customer’s annual opportunity to opt in to protection; no-knowledge / information-barrier units; riskless principal; ISO; odd-lot or bona fide error offsets; minimum price-improvement standards). This lesson teaches the main prohibition and that exceptions exist; it does **not** build a compliance engine, and it does **not** invent a “not-held” exception that is not in the current 5320 text.

Supplementary Material .08: members “generally may limit the life of a customer order to … 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time. However, if the customer and member agree to the processing of the customer’s order **outside normal market hours**, the protections of this Rule shall apply … at all times the customer order is executable by the member.” Extended-hours protection is not automatic unless the order is agreed to be executable then. A backtest of “the broker filled itself through my resting limit” is not how 5320 treats a held customer equity order. This complements Rule 606 “held” and 5310; no broker recommendation.


**FINRA 5270: front running of an imminent block transaction is a different prohibition from already-taught FINRA 5320: a member that has material, non-public market information about an imminent block may not cause an order in that security (or a related financial instrument) to be executed before that information has been made publicly available or has otherwise become stale or obsolete (checked 2026-11-12).** Already-taught 5320 (trading ahead of a held customer equity order, same-side, unless the customer is filled at the same or a better price) stays as written. This section adds official 5270 so a student does not treat 5320 as the only “trading ahead” rule, or treat a 10,000-share figure as a rigid floor. No product, broker, or venue recommendation.

[FINRA Rule 5270(a)](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5270): “No member or person associated with a member shall cause to be executed an order to buy or sell a security or a related financial instrument when such member or person associated with a member causing such order to be executed has material, non-public market information concerning an imminent block transaction in that security, a related financial instrument or a security underlying the related financial instrument prior to the time information concerning the block transaction has been made publicly available or has otherwise become stale or obsolete.” Supplementary Material .03: in equity securities, “a transaction involving 10,000 shares or more of a security, an underlying security, or a related financial instrument overlying such number of shares, is generally deemed to be a block transaction, although a transaction of fewer than 10,000 shares could be considered a block transaction.” “A block transaction that has been agreed upon does not lose its identity as such by arranging for partial executions of the full transaction in portions which themselves are not of block size if the execution of the full transaction may have a material impact on the market.” SM .02: information “shall be considered to be publicly available when it has been disseminated via a last sale reporting system” or the other official channels listed; “The requirement that information concerning the block transaction be made publicly available will not be satisfied until the entire block transaction has been completed and publicly reported.” SM .04 names permitted transactions (including unrelated trades, facilitating the customer block with consent, and a marketplace-rule / one-leg-on-exchange branch). SM .05: although 5270 is limited to imminent blocks, front running of other orders may still violate other FINRA rules, including Rule 2010 and already-taught Rule 5320. Name that official SM .04 exceptions exist; do not build an information-barrier / consent / exchange-leg engine.

Do not rewrite already-taught 5320, or treat 10,000 shares as a rigid minimum. Do not treat a print of part of a block as making the entire block “publicly available.”

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Backtesting fills at mid with zero schedule / impact.
2. Fitting η, γ on the evaluation path (look-ahead calibration).
3. Treating **above VWAP** as alpha rather than schedule benchmark noise.
4. Ignoring adverse selection when quoting limits.
5. Mixing arrival-price IS with close-to-close PnL without stating the benchmark.
6. Using public-tape IRFs as real metaorder costs without noting missing concavity/reversion.
7. Calibrating α / impact kernels as course edge.
8. Computing slippage against IEX quotes as if they were the NBBO, without stating the feed.
9. Reading a Rule 606 table as an execution-quality verdict, or a Rule 605 report as a routing and PFOF disclosure; quoting the withdrawn 2022 Order Competition Rule as if it were in force.
10. Treating the displayed NBBO size as all available liquidity, or inventing a dark-pool fill from the displayed quote; reading an off-exchange / TRF print as missing or bad data; or assuming every ATS publicly displays its best bid and offer.
11. Assuming every resting customer limit is in the displayed quote, or that hidden is the default under Rule 604; conflating 604 with Rule 606 “held” or with Rule 611 trade-through protection. Treating a broker filling itself through a resting customer equity order as if 5320 did not apply; inventing a “not-held” exception that is not in the current 5320 text; assuming extended-hours 5320 protection is automatic.
12. Treating 5250 as a rewrite of already-taught PFOF / Rule 606, or treating displayed quotes as proof the issuer paid a member to make a market; inventing a ban on every issuer-paid service, including underwriting fees or listing-fee reimbursement.
13. Treating 5270 as already-taught 5320; treating 10,000 shares as a rigid floor; treating a split into non-block pieces as if the full transaction were no longer a block; or treating a print of part of a block as making the entire block “publicly available.”
