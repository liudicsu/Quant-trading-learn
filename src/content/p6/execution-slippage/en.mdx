## Learning goals

This lesson (~**10 hours**: 3.5h concepts (incl √/Gatheral) + 5.5h experiments + 1h quiz) treats **execution as part of the strategy**: TWAP/VWAP schedules, Almgren–Chriss optimal liquidation, implementation shortfall, limit vs market, and arrival-price TCA. Prereqs: `costs-in-backtest`, `portfolio-risk-sizing`.

**Goals:** add participation / schedule discipline; write a one-page execution sheet tied to the shared cost model.

**Must-teach:** TWAP vs VWAP; Almgren & Chriss 2000/2001 *Journal of Risk* 3(2) — cost–risk efficient frontier, closed-form trajectory, risk-aversion (λ) sweep; Perold 1988 *JPM* 14(3) implementation shortfall; limit vs market fill-probability vs adverse-selection toy; slippage monitoring with an **arrival-price** benchmark and a post-trade report.

## Parameters

- Liquidation: `X0 = 1e6` shares over `T = 1` day, `N = 10` slices; daily `σ = 0.02`; temporary `η = 1e-6`; permanent `γ = 1e-7`; `S0 = 100`
- AC: `κ ≈ √(λ σ² / η)`; remaining shares `x(t) = X0 · sinh(κ(T−t)) / sinh(κT)`
- λ sweep: `logspace(-8, -3, 12)` → frontier `(E[cost], risk std)`
- Schedules: TWAP equal slices; VWAP matches a U-shaped expected volume profile
- Limit toy: half-spread 0.02, target edge 0.05, fill_p ≈ 0.55, 2000 sims

## TWAP and VWAP

**TWAP** spreads quantity evenly in time — ignores the volume profile. **VWAP** matches expected volume (here a U-shape: heavier open/close). Lab VWAP slices peak ≈ 1.61e5 at the wings vs TWAP 1.00e5 flat. Schedules use **ex-ante** volume forecasts only.

## Almgren & Chriss (2000/2001)

Almgren & Chriss (*Journal of Risk* 3(2), pp. 5–39; Dec 2000 working paper / 2001 issue) minimise expected cost plus a risk penalty from price volatility while shares remain. With linear temporary/permanent impact the **efficient frontier** of cost vs risk is explicit; higher risk-aversion λ → larger κ → faster (“urgent”) liquidation.

Lab λ sweep moves from patient (low E premium, higher inventory risk) to urgent (higher expected impact cost, lower risk). Trajectories for patient / balanced / urgent λ are plotted.

**Expected-value context:** AC optimises a *trader utility* over the execution window — it does not claim alpha. Choose λ from desk risk tolerance, not from backtest SR.

## Implementation shortfall (Perold 1988)

Perold (*Journal of Portfolio Management* 14(3), pp. 4–9) defines **implementation shortfall** as paper portfolio (decision / arrival price) minus realised execution. Lab sell programme (after fixing permanent impact to the **incremental** form `mid += dW − γ·n_j`): arrival 100, paper 1e8, realised ≈ 9.90e7, **IS ≈ 1.038e6 (~103.8 bps)** — matches the reviewer ~104 bps. Decompose delay vs trading (impact) under the arrival-price benchmark.

## Limit vs market (toy)

Market orders pay the half-spread for immediacy (lab mean cost 0.02). Limits improve price *conditional on fill* but add non-fill risk and **adverse selection** when fills cluster on moves against you. Lab: fill rate ≈ 0.56, limit mean PnL ≈ −0.028 under a simple adverse foil — limits are not free edge.

## Slippage monitoring / TCA mini-report

**Identity:** the *volume-weighted* arrival-price slippage equals Perold IS in bps — `Σ (arrival − exec)·n / (arrival·X0)`. Always report that quantity as IS.

On this path slice sizes are nearly equal, so the unweighted mean of per-slice arrival slips **coincides** with the volume-weighted Perold number (~103.8 bps). They diverge only when slice sizes differ — do not treat “unweighted ≠ Perold” as a universal claim.

**Interval VWAP** here uses the **planned** VWAP schedule weights (`vol_profile`), not realized tape volume. Lab volume-weighted benchmarks: **arrival/Perold ≈ 103.8 bps**, **planned-weight interval VWAP ≈ 100.0 bps**, **close ≈ 95.4 bps**. Export `examples/p6/data/tca_report.csv`.

## Look-ahead warnings

- Schedules and AC trajectories use only ex-ante σ, volume, and impact params
- Post-trade TCA may use the realised path — never feed realised fills back into the *decision* schedule without a clear sim protocol
- Impact parameters fitted on the same path you evaluate are circular

## Square-root / double-√ impact literacy

[arXiv:2502.16246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16246) (*The “double” square-root law*, TSE ID-resolved data) and companion [arXiv:2411.13965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13965) (strict √ universality on TSE) teach **mechanical** impact literacy:

- Child orders already obey √ in volume *after digestion*; mesoscopic metaorder √ emerges from a **double** square-root: √ volume × inverse-√ time decay.
- **Scramble foil:** reshuffling trader IDs yields synthetic metaorders with the **same** √Q law → impact looks mechanical, not “informed metaorder detection.”
- Schedule caveat: average impact is often roughly **independent of total time T** at moderate participation — not a free speed dial.
- Tension with **Almgren–Chriss linear η**: AC remains a desk **cost–risk utility** frontier; empirics favour concave √. Keep both: AC for trajectories, √ as the **capacity cost ruler**.

**Formula (cost ruler, not edge):** `I ≈ Y·σ·√(Q/V)`. **Reject** optimising Y on the evaluation path as “alpha.”

Lab toy (`Y=1`, `σ=0.02`, `V=1e6`):

| Q/V | √ impact (bps) | linear foil (bps) | √ / lin |
|-----|----------------|-------------------|---------|
| 0.001 | 6.32 | 0.20 | 31.6 |
| 0.01 | 20.00 | 2.00 | 10.0 |
| 0.05 | 44.72 | 10.00 | 4.47 |
| 0.10 | 63.25 | 20.00 | 3.16 |
| 0.20 | 89.44 | 40.00 | 2.24 |

Double-√ child cumulative (`q=1e4`, `i0=4`): i=1 → 4.72 bps … i=25 → 67.70 bps; plain metaorder √ at `Q=q·25` → 100 bps (N-dependent factor in the double-√ recovery — teaching sketch, not production calibration).

### Gatheral no-dynamic-arbitrage (one paragraph)

[Gatheral (2010), *Quantitative Finance* 10(7)](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697680903373692) (RePEc/DOI verified): no-dynamic-arbitrage links the **shape** of impact to its **decay**. In particular, **exponential decay is compatible only with linear impact** — another reason the AC linear-η + fast decay combo is a modelling tension next to empirical √. Literacy only — not an arb recipe.

<Callout type="warn" title="Debunk">
√ impact is a **cost ruler**. Do not ship “optimize Y → alpha,” Obizhaeva–Wang N-trader games, or RL execution papers as course edge.
</Callout>


## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Backtesting fills at mid with zero schedule / impact.
2. Fitting η, γ on the evaluation path (look-ahead calibration).
3. Treating VWAP beat as alpha rather than schedule benchmark noise.
4. Ignoring adverse selection when quoting limits.
5. Mixing arrival-price IS with close-to-close PnL without stating the benchmark.
