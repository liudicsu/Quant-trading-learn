## Parameters

- Liquidation: `X0 = 1e6` shares over `T = 1` day, `N = 10` slices; daily `σ = 0.02`; temporary `η = 1e-6`; permanent `γ = 1e-7`; `S0 = 100`
- AC: `κ ≈ √(λ σ² / η)`; remaining shares `x(t) = X0 · sinh(κ(T−t)) / sinh(κT)`
- λ sweep: `logspace(-8, -3, 12)` → frontier `(E[cost], risk std)`
- Schedules: TWAP equal slices; VWAP matches a U-shaped expected volume profile
- Limit toy: half-spread 0.02, target edge 0.05, fill_p ≈ 0.55, 2000 sims

## TWAP and VWAP

**TWAP** spreads quantity evenly in time — ignores the volume profile. **VWAP** matches expected volume (here a U-shape: heavier open/close). Lab VWAP slices peak ≈ 1.61e5 at the wings vs TWAP 1.00e5 flat. Schedules use **ex-ante** volume forecasts only.

## Almgren & Chriss (2000/2001)

Almgren & Chriss (*Journal of Risk* 3(2), pp. 5–39; Dec 2000 working paper / 2001 issue) minimise expected cost plus a risk penalty from price volatility while shares remain. With linear temporary/permanent impact the **efficient frontier** of cost vs risk is explicit; higher risk-aversion λ → larger κ → faster (“urgent”) liquidation.

Lab λ sweep moves from patient (low E premium, higher inventory risk) to urgent (higher expected impact cost, lower risk). Trajectories for patient / balanced / urgent λ are plotted.

**Expected-value context:** AC optimises a *trader utility* over the execution window — it does not claim alpha. Choose λ from desk risk tolerance, not from backtest SR.

## Implementation shortfall (Perold 1988)

Perold (*Journal of Portfolio Management* 14(3), pp. 4–9) defines **implementation shortfall** as paper portfolio (decision / arrival price) minus realised execution. Lab sell programme (after fixing permanent impact to the **incremental** form `mid += dW − γ·n_j`): arrival 100, paper 1e8, realised ≈ 9.90e7, **IS ≈ 1.038e6 (~103.8 bps)** — matches the reviewer ~104 bps. Decompose delay vs trading (impact) under the arrival-price benchmark.

## Limit vs market (toy)

Market orders pay the half-spread for immediacy (lab mean cost 0.02). Limits improve price *conditional on fill* but add non-fill risk and **adverse selection** when fills cluster on moves against you. Lab: fill rate ≈ 0.56, limit mean PnL ≈ −0.028 under a simple adverse foil — limits are not free edge.

## Slippage monitoring / TCA mini-report

**Identity:** the *volume-weighted* arrival-price slippage equals Perold IS in bps — `Σ (arrival − exec)·n / (arrival·X0)`. Always report that quantity as IS.

On this path slice sizes are nearly equal, so the unweighted mean of per-slice arrival slips **coincides** with the volume-weighted Perold number (~103.8 bps). They diverge only when slice sizes differ — do not treat “unweighted ≠ Perold” as a universal claim.

**Interval VWAP** here uses the **planned** VWAP schedule weights (`vol_profile`), not realized tape volume. Lab volume-weighted benchmarks: **arrival/Perold ≈ 103.8 bps**, **planned-weight interval VWAP ≈ 100.0 bps**, **close ≈ 95.4 bps**. Export `examples/p6/data/tca_report.csv`.

## Look-ahead warnings

- Schedules and AC trajectories use only ex-ante σ, volume, and impact params
- Post-trade TCA may use the realised path — never feed realised fills back into the *decision* schedule without a clear sim protocol
- Impact parameters fitted on the same path you evaluate are circular

## Square-root / double-√ impact literacy

[arXiv:2502.16246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16246) (*The “double” square-root law*, TSE ID-resolved data) and companion [arXiv:2411.13965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13965) (strict √ universality on TSE) teach **mechanical** impact literacy:

- Child orders already obey √ in volume *after digestion*; mesoscopic metaorder √ emerges from a **double** square-root: √ volume × inverse-√ time decay.
- **Scramble foil:** reshuffling trader IDs yields synthetic metaorders with the **same** √Q law → impact looks mechanical, not “informed metaorder detection.”
- Schedule caveat: average impact is often roughly **independent of total time T** at moderate participation — not a free speed dial.
- Tension with **Almgren–Chriss linear η**: AC remains a desk **cost–risk utility** frontier; empirics favour concave √. Keep both: AC for trajectories, √ as the **capacity cost ruler**.

**Formula (cost ruler, not edge):** `I ≈ Y·σ·√(Q/V)`. **Reject** optimising Y on the evaluation path as “alpha.”

Lab toy (`Y=1`, `σ=0.02`, `V=1e6`):

| Q/V | √ impact (bps) | linear foil (bps) | √ / lin |
|-----|----------------|-------------------|---------|
| 0.001 | 6.32 | 0.20 | 31.6 |
| 0.01 | 20.00 | 2.00 | 10.0 |
| 0.05 | 44.72 | 10.00 | 4.47 |
| 0.10 | 63.25 | 20.00 | 3.16 |
| 0.20 | 89.44 | 40.00 | 2.24 |

Double-√ child cumulative (`q=1e4`, `i0=4`): i=1 → 4.72 bps … i=25 → 67.70 bps; plain metaorder √ at `Q=q·25` → 100 bps (N-dependent factor in the double-√ recovery — teaching sketch, not production calibration).

### Gatheral no-dynamic-arbitrage (one paragraph)

[Gatheral (2010), *Quantitative Finance* 10(7), 749–759](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v10y2010i7p749-759.html) (RePEc/DOI verified): no-dynamic-arbitrage links the **shape** of impact to its **decay**. In particular, **exponential decay is compatible only with linear impact** — another reason the AC linear-η + fast decay combo is a modelling tension next to empirical √. Literacy only — not an arb recipe.

<Callout type="warn" title="Debunk">
√ impact is a **cost ruler**. Do not ship “optimize Y → alpha,” Obizhaeva–Wang N-trader games, or RL execution papers as course edge.
</Callout>




## Why public-data metaorder impact is hard (arXiv:2501.17096)

[arXiv:2501.17096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17096) (Naviglio et al.): when you build metaorder generalized IRFs from **public** price–trade models (TIM / Hasbrouck VAR / CNN–LSTM), the paths often disagree with **real metaorder** stylized facts.

| | Real metaorders (literature) | Public-data IRFs (paper's linear/nonlinear fits) |
|--|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| During execution | **Concave** growth (often near √t) | Near-**linear** (sometimes post-end inertia) |
| After the end | **Convex** partial reversion | **Weak** reversion / price may keep drifting |

**LMF literacy:** long memory in order flow ≈ many overlapping metaorders; your new child order does **not** trigger the same same-sign volume the unconditional VAR implies.

**Modified TIM α literacy:** α∈[0,1] = share of child volume that enters the **market volume** equation; α→1 near criticality → linear/inertia; smaller α closer to concave+revert. Lab toy (subcritical):

```
alpha | peak | end | revert_frac | below_chord_during
0.00 | 27.259 | 33.085 | -0.214 | +2.173
0.35 | 48.979 | 89.257 | -0.822 | +8.687
1.00 | 89.315 | 193.578 | -1.167 | +20.785
```

Higher α → weaker post-end reversion / more inertia risk (negative revert). **Protocol/warn: public tape often mis-estimates large metaorder impact.**

<Callout type="warn" title="Debunk">
Do **not** calibrate α on the evaluation path as alpha; no LOB HFT / Hawkes / GAN / MPC execution recipes. This paper is **protocol + warning**, not impact alpha.
</Callout>

## Which feed you watch changes your slippage (checked 2026-11-08)

Alpaca Basic real-time is IEX only ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/about-market-data-api)), while the NBBO comes from consolidated data (SIP). For the same fill, using the IEX mid or the SIP mid as the arrival price gives different slippage; a stale quote also distorts it. This is not “routing alpha”, just the wrong benchmark.

## Rule 605 vs FINRA 21-23: public statistics vs broker duty (checked 2026-11-08)

They do not overlap: **Rule 605** is the monthly public execution-quality **statistics** from market centers and larger brokers (calculation in `costs-in-backtest`, [SEC FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/trading-markets-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-rule-605-regulation-nms)); **FINRA 21-23** is about a broker's best-execution **duty** and regular reviews under Rule 5310, which is not only about price ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/21-23)). 605 data can be one input to such a review, but it covers only regular-hours covered orders, so it cannot replace your own arrival-price TCA or rank brokers.

## Rule 606: where your orders went, and what your broker was paid (checked 2026-11-10)

**Routing reports complement execution-quality reports.** Payment for order flow (PFOF) is cash or any other benefit a broker receives for routing customer orders to a particular market maker or exchange ([Rule 10b-10(d)(8)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-240.10b-10)). [Rule 606](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.606) requires every broker to publish a quarterly report on "non-directed" orders (the customer did not pick the venue) that were "held" (the broker must execute them promptly, with no discretion over price or time). Month by month, it lists the top ten venues and, for each venue, the net PFOF received or paid on market, marketable limit and non-marketable limit orders, both in total dollars and per share. S&P 500 stocks, other stocks and options get separate sections. The report is due within one month after the quarter ends and stays online for three years. A customer can also ask the broker for the routing of their own orders over the prior six months. Rule 605 (previous section) covers execution quality; 606 covers routing and money. Read them together. This lesson does not claim PFOF makes fills better or worse. Status: the SEC's 2022 Order Competition Rule and Regulation Best Execution proposals were formally withdrawn on 2025-06-12 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/06/order-competition-rule)); neither is in force.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Backtesting fills at mid with zero schedule / impact.
2. Fitting η, γ on the evaluation path (look-ahead calibration).
3. Treating **above VWAP** as alpha rather than schedule benchmark noise.
4. Ignoring adverse selection when quoting limits.
5. Mixing arrival-price IS with close-to-close PnL without stating the benchmark.
6. Using public-tape IRFs as real metaorder costs without noting missing concavity/reversion.
7. Calibrating α / impact kernels as course edge.
8. Computing slippage against IEX quotes as if they were the NBBO, without stating the feed.
9. Reading a Rule 606 table as an execution-quality verdict, or a Rule 605 report as a routing and PFOF disclosure; quoting the withdrawn 2022 Order Competition Rule as if it were in force.
