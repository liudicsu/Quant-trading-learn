## 2. Operational definition of a regime

Casual “bull / bear / chop” labels slide — three people can disagree on the same day. Engineering needs:

1. **Observable inputs:** only data available **before** decision time `t` (`shift(1)` / windows ending at `t−1`).
2. **Explicit rules:** e.g. “20-day MA above 60-day MA **and** lagged 20-day vol ≤ 1.5× lagged 60-day vol”.
3. **Label use:** drive **exposure caps, rebalance cadence, stress switches** — not a bullish/bearish slogan.

**Vol-bucket intuition:**

<Formula label="Lagged vol and spike flag">
{`σ̂_{t−1}^{(L)} = Std(r_{t−L}, …, r_{t−1});   spike_t = 1[ σ̂_{t−1}^{(20)} > 1.5 · σ̂_{t−1}^{(60)} ]`}
</Formula>

Weights / brakes at `t` may depend on lagged flags like `spike_t` only.

### HMM: Hamilton filter vs smoother (look-ahead warning)

[Hamilton (1989), *Econometrica* 57(2)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1912559) models unobserved states as a Markov switch and uses a **nonlinear filter** to infer “which state we may be in” from the observed series.

- **Filtered** `P(S_t | y_1,…,y_t)`: uses today and the past — **decision-eligible** (still a model, not truth).
- **Smoothed** `P(S_t | y_1,…,y_T)`: uses the **whole sample’s future** — fine for retrospective research; **live decisions = look-ahead leakage**.

Lab D (toy two-state Gaussian with known params; first stress planted at `t=80`):

| Time | Filtered P(stress) | Smoothed P(stress) |
|------|-------------------:|-------------------:|
| `t=79` (day before onset) | **0.022** | **0.176** |
| `t=80` (onset day) | 0.092 | 0.439 |

The smoother rises **before** the crash is fully in the filter’s information set — that is the shape of leakage.

![HMM filter vs smoother](/p6/regime_hmm_filter_vs_smooth.png)

Pretty “perfect turning points” on research charts often come from smoothing. Production labels / sizing rules use filtered probs (or simpler `shift(1)` rules) only.
</Callout>

## 4. Four scenario playbooks

Each playbook has three columns: **identification (causal) → engineering response → failure modes**. Responses are a risk menu, not a “guaranteed profit” list.

### A. One-way rally / fear of a pullback

- **ID:** lagged dual-MA uptrend; shallow DD bucket; no vol spike.
- **Engineering:** scheduled rebalance; vol-target scales exposure with `σ̂_{t−1}`; optional trend overlay; widen a **no-trade band** when costs are high (see `portfolio-risk-sizing`).
- **Failure:** end-of-trend whipsaw; “fear of pullback” → chronic de-risking → costs dominate; treating a news day as a regime switch.

### B. Ordinary pullback vs crisis

- **Pullback ID:** price off highs by a few percent, but lagged correlations calm, vol moderate, liquidity narrative normal.
- **Crisis ID:** deep DD bucket + vol spike + **corr→1** + liquidity worsening (spreads/impact).
- **Engineering:** pullback — keep existing brakes/rebalance discipline; crisis — up-weight stress scenarios, tighten leverage/concentration, hard DD kill (`risk-systems-lab`).
- **Failure:** promoting every −3% day to “crisis playbook” → permanently low risk; or denying crisis features → diversification illusion.

### C. Crash crisis

**Teaching stress table (order-of-magnitude literacy — not a forecast, not your backtest alpha):**

| Teaching tag | Common classroom narrative | Engineering association |
|--------------|----------------------------|-------------------------|
| 1987-10 | Extreme single-day drop | Gap risk; continuous-trading assumptions break |
| 2000–02 | Long post-bubble bear | Crowded valuation + slow bleed |
| 2008 | Credit/liquidity spiral | Corr→1, funding stress |
| 2020-03 | Fast crash then policy response | Instant liquidity vacuum |
| 2022 | Rate-shock repricing | Equity–bond joint drawdowns |

**Mechanism literacy (not signals):**

- **US market-wide circuit breakers (MWCB):** NYSE Rule 7.12 et al. trigger off S&P 500 decline vs prior close — **7% / 13% / 20%** (Levels 1/2/3); Levels 1/2 before 15:25 typically halt ~15 minutes; Level 3 halts trading until **4:00 a.m. ET or later on the following trading day** (NYSE, Nasdaq and other exchanges' rule text since autumn 2026; it used to say "the rest of the day"; see the MWCB section below; confirm live exchange rules; learning use).
- **A-share price limits:** limit-up/down change path shape; backtests that ignore limits overstate fillable size.
- **2016 China index circuit-breaker trial:** after rapid controversy that halts accelerated selling, the CSRC suspended the mechanism effective 2016-01-08 (SSE English notice / CSRC statements) — **suspension itself teaches: mechanisms change behaviour; they are not alpha switches**.

**CPPI literacy:** cushion-based dynamic exposure; classic failures are **gap risk** (jump through the floor) and **cash-lock** after a floor hit (miss the rebound) — **not a holy grail**.

**Corr→1:** Lab C equal four-asset toy: calm mean off-diagonal corr ≈ **0.069**; stress window ≈ **0.990**; common −8% shock day book PnL ≈ **−0.0776** (≈ single-name crash). Diversification shrinks in crises.

### D. Range-bound (chop)

- **ID:** dual MAs tangled; price oscillates in a band; vol moderate.
- **Engineering:** down-weight trend overlays; tighten no-trade bands; book turnover costs honestly.
- **Failure:** trend rules **whipsaw** — signals “work,” net NAV grinds down after costs.

## 5. Cross-scenario hard rules

1. **Every state input is lagged** (`shift(1)` / window ends at `t−1`).
2. **Brakes and limits before opinions** — opinions can be wrong; hard constraints must still execute (ties to `risk-systems-lab`).
3. **Stress tests are board-level narrative checks, not probability forecasts.**
4. **Circuit breakers / price limits / suspensions = microstructure, ≠ trade signals.**
5. **Filtered OK; smoothed banned for decisions.**
6. **When the brief is risk metrics only, do not smuggle end-NAV as “the strategy won.”**

## 8. Risk box: options & VIX basics (risks only, no returns)

<Callout type="warn" title="Options/VIX are not free insurance">
**Option premium (OIC official education pages):** [OIC](https://www.optionseducation.org/optionsoverview/leverage-risk) explains that the price an option buyer pays is the premium; options provide leverage, and if the underlying does not move as anticipated, leverage can magnify the percentage loss; if the options expire with no value, the buyer’s loss can be **the entire premium paid**; **an uncovered option writer may face unlimited risk**. OIC also explains that buying puts can act like insurance against a price drop, at the cost of the premium ([source](https://www.optionseducation.org/optionsoverview/what-are-the-benefits-risks)).

**P&L including premium (hand-calc toy):** hold a stock at 100 and buy one put with strike 95 and premium 2 (at expiry):
- Expiry price 80: put worth 15, minus premium 2 → option leg +13 (stock leg −20, total −7 rather than −20).
- Expiry price 100: put worth 0 → option leg −2, **the whole premium is lost**.
Protection has a cost, and you pay it every time even when no drop happens.

**VIX ≠ something you can buy directly:** the [Cboe VIX page](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/vix/) explains that the VIX Index reflects expected volatility implied by SPX option prices; VIX futures have traded on Cboe Futures Exchange (CFE) since 2004, and VIX options are separate contracts. The [Cboe VIX FAQ](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/vix/faqs/) specifically explains why the spot VIX value and the settlement VIX value differ. So: **the index rising does not mean the futures/options/related product you hold rises the same way**.

**This lesson’s stance:** risk literacy only. No hedge-return promise of any kind, no recommendation of any option or VIX product; the labs in this lesson do not use options.
</Callout>

### 8b. US equity options: the minimum a quant learner needs (checked 2026-11-10)

Before buying or selling an option, US investors must read the **Options Disclosure Document** (ODD, "Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options"); the current version is dated **June 2024** ([OCC ODD page](https://www.theocc.com/company-information/documents-and-archives/options-disclosure-document), [official PDF](https://www.theocc.com/getcontentasset/dd6200a7-5982-4226-90e4-1f2d32a89911/dfc3d011-8f63-43f6-9ed8-4b444333a1d0/june_2024_riskstoc.pdf), checked 2026-11-10). The points below quote it, with printed page numbers.

- **Contract unit:** "the unit of trading for most options on equity securities is 100 shares" (p.8). A quoted premium of $4 per share costs **$400 per contract**. Beginners often get this wrong by a factor of 100.
- **Exercise style:** an American-style option can be exercised at any time before expiration; as of the June 2024 ODD, every European-style option then traded can be exercised only on its expiration date (p.7).
- **Assignment:** a seller (writer) can be assigned, meaning forced to fulfil the contract. An uncovered writer can face very large losses (see the OIC note above).
- **Dividends:** "As a general rule, no adjustment is made for ordinary cash dividends" (p.18). So near the ex-dividend date (see `data-cleaning`), a call holder may exercise early to collect the dividend, and the ODD says a call writer's chance of being assigned "may increase" as the ex-date approaches (p.19). The lab's "extrinsic value below the dividend" flag is a teaching rule of thumb, not an ODD rule.
- **Settlement:** since 2024-05-28, physically settled stock options settle on the first business day after exercise (T+1) (p.57).

Outside the ODD: OCC's [standard equity option specifications](https://www.theocc.com/clearance-and-settlement/clearing/equity-options-product-specifications) list the exercise style as **American-style**; index options may be European-style and cash-settled, so always read the contract specification (checked 2026-11-10).

**Advanced: two pieces of theory, used only as references.**

- **Put-call parity** (European options, no dividends; Stoll 1969): `C - P = S - K * exp(-r * T)`. It comes from no-arbitrage alone, with no model. American options satisfy only inequalities, not this equality.
- **Black-Scholes-Merton** (Black & Scholes 1973; Merton 1973) gives a reference price under constant volatility, lognormal prices and no frictions. When the model price and the market price differ, that is a sign the assumptions do not hold, **not** a "mispricing" or alpha. Implied volatility is backed out of option prices: the ODD calls it a reflection of the market's predictions about future volatility, and realized volatility may not match it. Treat it as a market-implied estimate with no guarantee, not as a trading signal.

## Three US “brakes”: MWCB / LULD / Reg SHO 201 (checked 2026-11-08)

MWCB (market-wide circuit breakers) is already on the site; this adds the single-stock LULD and the short-sale price test, side by side.

| Layer | Trigger | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| MWCB | Large one-day drop in the market index | Market-wide trading halt |
| LULD | A stock hits its dynamic price band and the Limit State is not exited within 15 seconds | The primary listing exchange usually declares a 5-minute pause (can be extended) |
| Reg SHO 201 | A stock falls ≥10% from the prior day’s close | Short-sale price restriction for the rest of the day and the next day ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/subject-group-ECFR1607681c7b4f78d/section-242.201)) |
| Overnight price bands (Phase 1, planned 2026-12-06) | During Overnight Protected Hours (Sun–Thu 21:00 to 04:00 ET next day) a stock trades/quotes outside its overnight band | Trading centers must prevent out-of-band trades and displayed quotes; **no** automatic pause, the Primary Listing Exchange may declare a Regulatory Halt ([SEC 34-106042](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-106042.pdf)) |

**LULD tiers (per review item R12):** LULD is not a fixed all-day limit relative to the prior close. The bands sit around a dynamic reference price; the applicable percentage tier is set for the day, per the Plan, from the prior trading day’s primary-market closing price and similar: in the ordinary non-leveraged example, for a price tier above $3 Tier 1 is 5% and Tier 2 is 10%; from $0.75 to $3 (inclusive) both are 20%; below $0.75 the band is the lesser of $0.15 or 75% of the reference price. In the last 25 minutes before the regular close, only all Tier 1 stocks and Tier 2 stocks with a price tier not above $3 use doubled parameters; on early-close days it is the last 25 minutes before the early close. Tier 2 stocks above $3 are not uniformly doubled at the close. Amendment 18, implemented 2020-02-24, removed the 09:30–09:45 New York opening doubling for all stocks; do not repeat the old description. ETF/ETP tiering and leveraged products must be checked against the current official list/Plan, not guessed from the ticker name.

The reference price is usually the mean of eligible trades over the prior 5 minutes; opening/reopening have special provisions. The lab keeps two inputs apart: the **prior trading day’s primary-listing close** sets the day’s price tier (percentage and closing-doubling eligibility), while the **moving reference price** does not reselect the day’s price tier; it sets the centre and is used with the fixed tier parameters to calculate the width and bounds (LULD Plan §V(A): the Price Bands are calculated by applying the Percentage Parameter to the Reference Price, the Lower Price Band below it and the Upper Price Band above it; Amendment 20 text read by the author on 2026-11-08). The synthetic state machine below is **not a complete rule engine** and covers ordinary non-leveraged stocks only.

**Reference-price update threshold and 30-second hold (LULD Plan Amendment 20 §V(A)(2), original text read by the author on 2026-11-08):** the Processor calculates a Pro-Forma Reference Price on a continuous basis during Regular Trading Hours; if it “has not moved by 1% or more from the Reference Price currently in effect, no new Price Bands shall be disseminated, and the current Reference Price shall remain the effective Reference Price.” When it has moved by 1% or more, it becomes the Reference Price and new Price Bands are disseminated, “provided, however, that each new Reference Price shall remain in effect for at least 30 seconds.” The same 1% / 30-second rule applies in the period less than five minutes after the Opening Price (§V(B)(1)). **This lesson’s toy does not model the 1% threshold or the 30-second hold**: it takes the input reference price as the one currently in effect.

## Overnight LULD price bands (Phase 1, checked 2026-11-08)

**Status:** LULD Plan Amendment No. 27 (SEC 34-106042, approved 2026-08-05) adds overnight protections, expected to start on 2026-12-06, subject to systems changes by the SIP Processors ([SEC 34-106042](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-106042.pdf)). This is Phase 1 of two.

- **Hours:** Sunday–Thursday 21:00 ET to 04:00 ET the next day (Overnight Protected Hours).
- **Two reference prices** (adjusted for corporate actions): the listing market's official Closing Price; the consolidated last round-lot sale at 19:30 ET. The 19:30 time comes from Amendment No. 29 (was 19:45; in effect **temporarily, for no more than 120 days**, out for comment, [SEC 34-106624](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-106624.pdf)).
- **Bands:** `lower = min(low_ref × 0.8, low_ref − min_dist)`, `upper = max(high_ref × 1.2, high_ref + min_dist)`; minimum distance: $3 if the Closing Price is $1 or more, $1 if it is below $1. For leveraged ETPs both the 20% and the minimum are multiplied by the leverage ratio.
- **Timeline:** sent to the Processors by 20:55, published before 21:00.
- **Hitting a band:** trading centers must prevent out-of-band trades and displayed quotes; there is **no** automatic Limit State → pause as in regular hours. The Primary Listing Exchange may declare a Regulatory Halt; during it orders are rejected, and the stock does not reopen in that night's Overnight Protected Hours.
- It is **not** limit-up/limit-down and does **not** guarantee a fill. This lesson says nothing about Phase 2.

## MWCB Level 3 under 23/5 trading (checked 2026-11-10)

Exchange overnight sessions are planned from 2026-12-06 (see above). The old MWCB text said a Level 3 halt lasts "for the remainder of the trading day". Under 23/5 trading a reader could conclude that trading reopens in the 21:00 ET overnight session the same evening. **That is wrong.**

- NYSE filed SR-NYSE-2026-43, effective on filing, replacing that phrase with: trading halts "until 4:00 a.m. ET or later on the following trading day". NYSE says this is "not intended to make any substantive changes to the MWCB mechanism" and that the other SROs intend to file the same change. NYSE's old rule already resumed no earlier than 7:00 a.m. ET for UTP securities, so "or later" matters ([SEC 34-106380](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nyse/2026/34-106380.pdf)).
- Nasdaq filed the same change to its Rule 4121 (SR-NASDAQ-2026-080, effective on filing), noting its Night Session would otherwise allow a 21:00 ET reopening ([SEC 34-106586](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nasdaq/2026/34-106586.pdf)).
- As of 2026-11-10, the same change had also been filed, immediately effective, by the rest of the NYSE group (Arca, American, National, Texas), Nasdaq PHLX and Nasdaq Texas, the four Cboe equity exchanges (e.g. SR-CboeBZX-2026-077, [SEC 34-106582](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboebzx/2026/34-106582.pdf)), MEMX, MIAX Pearl, IEX, LTSE and MX2. No such filing was found for FINRA, Nasdaq BX, NYSE Chicago or 24X, so do **not** write "the whole market has one rule". Each venue sets its own operative date (Nasdaq says it will coordinate with other SROs); check each venue's current rulebook.
- The 7% / 13% / 20% levels and the 3:25 p.m. rule for Levels 1/2 are unchanged.

## SEC trading suspension vs exchange halt (checked 2026-11-11)

The three brakes above are **exchange or plan mechanisms**: rules that pause trading when prices move too far. A different tool belongs to the SEC itself.

- **What it is.** Under Exchange Act Section 12(k)(1)(A), the SEC may by order "summarily to suspend trading in any security ... for a period not exceeding 10 business days" when it believes the public interest and the protection of investors require it ([15 U.S.C. 78l(k)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/78l)). The SEC keeps a public list of these orders ([SEC Trading Suspensions](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/trading-suspensions)).
- **A recent example.** SEC Release 34-106668 suspended trading in the securities of a holding company incorporated in the British Virgin Islands and headquartered in Hong Kong, from 4:00 a.m. ET on 2026-10-15 to 11:59 p.m. ET on 2026-10-28. The stated reason: potential manipulation through recommendations made by unknown persons via social media to buy, hold or sell the stock and to send screenshots of their trades, which "appear to be designed to artificially inflate the price and trading volume" ([order PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/suspensions/2026/34-106668-ts.pdf)). The SEC list shows several similar orders in 2025 and 2026. This lesson names no stock and makes no claim about any company beyond the order's own words.
- **Times in Beijing.** 2026-10-15 04:00 ET (daylight time) is 16:00 Beijing time the same day; 2026-10-28 23:59 ET is 11:59 Beijing time on 2026-10-29.

| | Exchange halt / LULD pause / MWCB | SEC trading suspension |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Listing exchange or the NMS plans | The SEC, by order |
| Why | Price moves, pending news, regulatory reasons | Public interest and investor protection (for example, possible manipulation or doubts about public information) |
| How long | LULD/MWCB: minutes, up to the next trading day (MWCB Level 3). Exchange regulatory halts (news pending, doubts about listing standards): the exchange, not the SEC, decides, and the [SEC investor glossary](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/trading-halts-and-delays) gives no maximum length, so do not assume it ends the same day; check the exchange's halt and resumption notices | Up to 10 business days |

**Backtest rule:** load the SEC suspension list as a dated event table and mark every row inside a suspension window as **not tradable** (the mask-first idea from `data-cleaning`); do not forward-fill a price as if you could trade it, and expect a gap when trading resumes. A price and volume spike that the SEC links to possible manipulation is not a momentum pattern to learn from; flag it, do not feed it to a model as normal data. Learning only: this is not legal advice and not a list of stocks to avoid or buy.

## Common mistakes / traps

1. Computing today’s state from today’s close, then trading the same bar (look-ahead).
2. Putting smoothed HMM probs on a “live” dashboard.
3. Trading the circuit-breaker print.
4. Promoting every ordinary pullback to the crisis playbook.
5. Treating stress-table years as strategy return promises.
6. Running trend rules in chop without costs.
7. Mapping a news “correction” straight onto the course regime enum.
8. Treating a bought put as “free insurance” and ignoring the premium; treating moves in the VIX Index as moves in VIX futures/options/related products.
9. Treating LULD as a fixed 10% limit vs the prior close; repeating the removed 09:30–09:45 opening doubling; assuming every Tier 2 doubles at the close.
10. Treating overnight price bands as limit-up/down or a fill guarantee; assuming an overnight band hit triggers an automatic 5-minute pause as in regular hours; treating the 19:30 reference time as permanent (Amendment No. 29 is in effect temporarily).
11. Reading a $4 option quote as $4 per contract instead of $400; treating a BSM model-vs-market gap as alpha; forgetting that ordinary dividends do not adjust option terms; assuming a Level 3 circuit breaker reopens in the same evening's overnight session.
12. Treating an SEC trading suspension as an ordinary exchange halt that ends the same day; forward-filling prices through a suspension window; training a model on a price spike the SEC linked to possible manipulation as if it were normal momentum.
