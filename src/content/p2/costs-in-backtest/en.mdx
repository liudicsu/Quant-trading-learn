## Learning goals

This lesson (~**6.75 hours**: 2h concepts + 3.5h coding + 0.5h quiz + 0.75h Rule 605 effective spread) splits friction into **four parts**, adds a **square-root impact** capacity demo, and keeps the combined bps ladder. Prereq: `backtest-traps`.

**Goals:** implement each cost with a numeric example; stacked breakdown; capital capacity chart.

**Must-teach:** zero-cost ≠ live evidence; commission vs half-spread vs slippage vs impact.

<Callout type="info" title="Learning only — not investment advice">
Toy bps and a pedagogical √ impact — not a broker schedule or guaranteed TCA.
</Callout>

## Parameters

- MA10, `position=shift(1)`, Rf=0, √252
- Commission **2 bps** one-way; half-spread **5 bps**; slippage **3 bps**
- Impact: `k * sigma * sqrt(Q/ADV)` with k=**0.10**, ADV=**1e6** shares, σ from the panel
- Base capital **USD 1e6** for the stacked path; capacity grid from **USD 1e5** to **USD 5e7**

## Four parts (each with a number)

1. **Commission** — explicit fee: 2 bps → 0.0002 return one-way.
2. **Half bid–ask spread** — crossing half the quote: 5 bps → 0.0005.
3. **Slippage** — extra adverse move beyond half-spread: 3 bps → 0.0003.
4. **Market impact (square-root)** — pedagogical `k * sigma * sqrt(Q/ADV)`. For Q/ADV=0.05 and σ≈0.0147: impact≈**0.000329** (~3.3 bps).

<Callout type="info" title="Citation: Almgren et al. 2005">
[Almgren, Thum, Hauptmann & Li (2005), *Direct Estimation of Equity Market Impact*](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/costestim.pdf) discuss the square-root candidate for temporary impact and, on their Citigroup desk sample, prefer a **3/5** power in participation rate (rejecting exact β=1/2 at 95%). This lesson still teaches the common **√** reduced form for capacity intuition and cites that paper for the empirical debate.
</Callout>

Demo total one-way (2+5+3 bps + √ impact) ≈ **13.3 bps**.

**Bridge (deepened in `execution-slippage`):** the double-√ / mechanical √ impact literacy of [arXiv:2502.16246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16246) (+ companion [2411.13965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13965)) — same `I≈Yσ√(Q/V)` ruler spirit; do not treat Y as alpha.

## Impact volatility (no look-ahead)

Path √impact uses **lagged rolling** daily vol: `rolling(20).std().shift(1)`, with warm-up = expanding std also shifted. The single-trade numeric demo uses a **stated prior** σ from the first 100 bars only — not the full-sample std.

## Continuous positions × turnover

Once positions are **continuous** (not just 0/1 flips), scale impact (and other one-way frictions) by **turnover** `|Δposition|` each bar — not by a single Q/ADV computed from capital alone. The lab’s impact series already multiplies `turnover * (capital/price) / ADV`; keep that link when you move beyond binary holdings.

## Calibrating half-spread with amended Rule 605 (checked 2026-11-08)

**What it is:** Rule 605 requires market centers and larger brokers (100,000 or more customer accounts) to publish monthly order-execution-quality reports. The amended rule's compliance date is 2026-08-01; detailed monthly reports are pipe-delimited ASCII files with 55 fields, free to download, and the summary report uses the OES CSV schema (SEC staff FAQ, [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/trading-markets-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-rule-605-regulation-nms)). This lesson **did not read the field-definition source, so it names no fields**.

**Effective spread (17 CFR §242.600(b)(8); FAQ Question 27, Example 27-1):** keep the **direction sign**: for a buy `ES = 2 × (exec_price − mid)`, for a sell `ES = 2 × (mid − exec_price)`. A negative value means the fill landed on the customer's favourable side of the mid; it is not a "negative fee". This example is a market order, so the mid is taken at receipt; other order types use other benchmark times set by the rule (e.g. midpoint-or-better limit orders use the time they first become executable). Example: a market order to buy 3000 shares is received at 3:30 p.m., NBBO 25.30 / 25.60, mid 25.45; 400 shares fill at 25.50 (0.10), 1000 at 25.55 (0.20), 1600 at 25.60 (0.30). Weighted by **executed shares** (FAQ Question 37), the average is $0.24.

**Converting to a percentage (§242.600(b)(10)):** the official measure is share-weighted average ES ÷ share-weighted average mid, **not** the average of per-order bps. With different mids the two give different numbers (see lab part C).

**Three pitfalls (605 data ≠ your real cost):**

1. **Covered orders only:** generally orders received during regular trading hours while an NBBO is disseminated (Q9). Two exceptions are included: a **non-marketable limit order (NMLO) received pre-open**, for the remainder that becomes executable in regular hours (Q28); and an **NMLO received during a halt**, once it first becomes executable after trading resumes (Q20). Being in the report does not mean an ES is computed for it: plain NMLOs get no ES (Q30).
2. **Special handling excluded:** e.g. fill-or-kill and average-price orders (Q11); non-IOC pegged orders (Q14); directed orders excluded at the receiving broker (Q12); market/marketable limit orders received while the NBB or NBO is outside the LULD bands (Q16); an NBBO crossed for 30 seconds or more (Q19); market/marketable limit orders received during a halt, and orders received **less than 5 minutes** before a halt (for NMLOs, first executable) that are still outstanding when it starts (Q20).
3. **Regular hours only:** executions outside regular trading hours are excluded (Q28), so pre-market, after-hours and overnight costs are **invisible**. Keep three times apart: receipt, first executable, execution.

It is an **aggregate** over the reporter's covered orders, not your own fill cost and not a broker ranking. Use it only to sanity-check the **order of magnitude** of this lesson's 5 bps half-spread.

**Timeline:** price improvement versus the best available displayed price is collected from 2026-11-01, and November reports are due by the end of December 2026 (Q39).

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Posting zero-cost equity curves as live evidence.
2. Collapsing spread, slippage, and impact into one unlabeled “cost.”
3. Treating toy bps as an exchange’s official tariff.
4. Scaling capital without rescaling Q/ADV in the impact term.
5. Changing the signal after seeing the cost ladder (leakage).
6. Treating a Rule 605 effective spread as your own real cost; writing the signed effective spread as an absolute value, or passing off a mean of per-order bps as the official percentage; forgetting it covers only covered orders (with the NMLO exceptions) and excludes special-handling orders; guessing field names without reading the field-definition source.
