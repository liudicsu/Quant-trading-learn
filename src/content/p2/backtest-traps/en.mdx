## Cheat vs honest (look-ahead)

Same MA10 rule: **cheat** uses today’s close in the signal **and** today’s close-to-close return; **honest** shifts the position by one day. Lab: cheat Sharpe≈**5.27** / NAV≈**2.29** vs honest≈**−1.12** / **0.82**.

## Recap: survivorship & data snooping

Cross-link: [data-cleaning](/en/phase/data-honest-backtests/data-cleaning).

- **Survivorship:** today’s listed names ≠ the historical investable universe — replaying only survivors inflates means (numeric demo lives in data-cleaning).
- **Data snooping:** choosing a rule after many unlabeled full-sample peeks / rank picks. Walk-forward + trial logs + DSR exist to make that cost visible.

## Walk-forward lab

Rolling windows pick the best MA length on each **train** block, then score the next **test** block without refitting inside the test.

Lab summary (10 folds): mean train SR≈**0.25**, mean test SR≈**−0.89**, median test≈**−1.75**, fraction of test SR>0 ≈**0.30**. Per-fold table is in the run output below.

## Why some walk-forward test Sharpes are NaN

Folds **6, 7, 9** print `sharpe_test=NaN` because the test window’s position path is **flat** (PnL≈0 → sample std=0), so Sharpe is undefined. The summary **mean/median skip those NaN folds** (pandas default); `frac_test_positive` treats NaN as not >0.

## False hits under the null (context for mean_false_hits≈2.78)

With n=**299** returns, under a Normal null the SE of the **annualized** Sharpe is ≈ √(252/299) ≈ **0.918**. Then P(|SR|>1.5) ≈ **0.102**, so among **30** trials the expected hit count is ≈ **3.07**. The lab’s Monte Carlo mean_false_hits≈**2.78** (50 seeds) sits close to that; the small gap is expected from ±1 discrete signs vs Gaussian noise.

## Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)

From Bailey & López de Prado (2014), *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio*: DSR is a **PSR whose threshold is the expected maximum SR under multiple testing**. Under a null with mean 0 and cross-trial σ_SR:


```text
E[\max SR]\approx \sigma_{SR}\Big[(1-\gamma)\Phi^{-1}(1-\tfrac1N)+\gamma\,\Phi^{-1}(1-\tfrac1{Ne})\Big]
```


with Euler–Mascheroni γ≈0.5772. Then `mathrm{DSR}=widehat{PSR}(SR^*=E[max])`. As **N** grows, E[max] rises and **DSR falls** — even for a fixed selected trial.

Lab (selected train-best w=35): PSR(0)≈**0.42**; DSR drops from ≈**0.42** (N=1) toward ≈**0.06** (N=200). Chart: `/p2/dsr_vs_N.png`.

![DSR vs N](/p2/dsr_vs_N.png)

## Combinatorial purged CV (CPCV) teaching sketch

**Intuition:** A single train/hold-out split gives you **one** out-of-sample Sharpe. If that window is lucky or unlucky, you may love or hate a rule for the wrong reason. Combinatorial purged cross-validation (AFML ch.12) cuts the timeline into \(N\) contiguous groups, takes every combination of \(k\) groups as the test block, purges train labels that overlap those tests, and rebuilds several backtest **paths**. You then read the **distribution** of path metrics.

**Counts:** combinations \(C(N,k)\); paths \(\varphi[N,k]=C(N-1,k-1)\). Lab: \(N=6\), \(k=2\) → \(C(6,2)=15\) combinations and \(\varphi=C(5,1)=5\) paths.

**Numeric toy** (honest MA pnl): combo test Sharpes mean ≈ **−0.82**, std ≈ **1.44**; a single 2/3–1/3 hold-out yields **one** OOS SR ≈ **−1.34**. The cloud is the point. Paths **reuse** test groups — they are **not** independent DSR trials.

![CPCV SR distribution](/p2/cpcv_sr_dist.png)

**Disclaimer:** this demo is a **teaching sketch**, not production AFML `CombinatorialPurgedKFold`. The same combinatorial spirit reappears when `ml-leakage-control` scores many purged folds.

## One-switch decision-time leakage (TEMP_CENTER / EXEC_OPEN)

Chronological train/test splits can still be wrong. [When Alpha Disappears](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23959) (Zhang et al.) builds a **one-switch** benchmark: hold the panel, walk-forward split, model family, horizon, portfolio rule, and cost convention fixed — then flip **one** protocol rule and measure **Leakage Gain** = metric(leaky) − metric(CLEAN).

**Intuition:** “Out-of-sample” only means the *calendar* is later. It does **not** mean the *information set* at decision time was legal.

Two switches dominate their tables:

| Switch | What breaks | Clean alternative |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **TEMP_CENTER** | Centered rolling features use future observations around \(t\) | Trailing rolling only |
| **EXEC_OPEN** | Signal uses same-day post-open fields (H/L/C/V) while assuming a same-day **open** fill | Decide after close \(t\); enter at open \(t+1\) |

Lab toy (diagnostic inflation, **not** deployable alpha):

```text
CLEAN SR@toy≈-0.741
TEMP_CENTER SR≈6.265  LeakageGain_SR≈+7.006
EXEC_OPEN   SR≈20.816  LeakageGain_SR≈+21.556
```

Large positive Leakage Gain means the **protocol** made the future easier to see — not that the model suddenly became a genius. Paper also finds NORM_GLOBAL / STRUCT_GRAPH / EXEC_CLOSE often weak. Short reuse note lives in `ml-leakage-control`.


## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Same-bar fills labeled “research.”
2. Reporting the best of many trials without N.
3. Interpreting equal honest NAV tricks as anti-look-ahead proof.
4. Ignoring survivorship while “fixing” on today’s index members.
5. Treating PSR/DSR thresholds as live trading rules without reading the papers’ assumptions (IID / stationary ergodic returns, independent trials).
6. Counting CPCV paths as independent DSR trials \(N\).
7. Treating the teaching CPCV sketch as production AFML code.
8. Trusting a chronological split while TEMP_CENTER / EXEC_OPEN still peek.
