## Stop, stop-limit, and trailing stop: the stop is not a guaranteed fill (checked 2026-11-12)

A **stop order** (sometimes called a stop-loss) becomes a **market order** when the stop price is reached. The official beginner primer is the investor.gov bulletin “[Stop, Stop-Limit, and Trailing Stop Orders](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-15)”, updated **2026-08-18**. It is a **staff bulletin**, not a Commission rule.

“The stop price is not the guaranteed execution price for a stop order.” The stop is only a trigger. The fill on the resulting market order can **deviate significantly** from the stop. A short-term intraday move can produce a fill “substantially worse than the stock’s closing price for the day.” A gap through the stop, a trading halt, or a wick that touches the stop is not a fill at the stop price. Do not backtest a stop-loss as a guaranteed fill at the stop.

A **stop-limit** becomes a **limit** order once the stop is reached, so it “may not be executed if the stock’s price moves away from the specified limit price.” The stop and the limit need not be the same price (the bulletin’s example: a sell stop of $3.00 with a limit of $2.50).

A **trailing stop** trails in the favorable direction and holds still in the unfavorable direction. The bulletin’s example: you buy at $20; the price rises to $24; a $1 sell trailing stop holds at $23; “the execution price may deviate from $23.”

Trigger convention — **last-sale versus quotation** — “differ[s]” by firm. Check with the broker; do not assume last sale. Availability of these order types is firm-specific. This lesson does not recommend any order type, broker, or venue.

## US main line: settlement, accounts, quotes and sessions (checked 2026-11-08)

**Default scope:** US-listed stocks, USD. This is mechanism literacy, not broker rules or investment advice.

- **T+1:** since 2024-05-28 most US securities trades settle **one business day** after the trade date ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-62)). It is not “24 hours”, and it does not mean you cannot sell the same day. Weekends and exchange holidays are not business days.

- **Cash vs margin account:** a cash account buys with settled funds; a margin account borrows from the broker and has maintenance margin. The PDT successor rule and its phase-in are in `compliance-ethics` (not repeated here).
- **Freeriding in a cash account (checked 2026-11-11):** under Regulation T, a cash-account customer agrees to make full cash payment for a security **before selling it** ([12 CFR 220.8(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/section-220.8)). If you sell (or deliver the shares to another broker) before you have paid in full, the privilege of delaying payment beyond the trade date is withdrawn for **90 calendar days** after the sale ([220.8(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/section-220.8)). During that freeze you may still buy, but you must pay in full on the trade date ([investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/freeriding)). The freeze does not apply if, within the payment period, full payment is received (or a check clears) and you do not withdraw the sale proceeds first. "Payment period" is the standard US settlement cycle **plus two business days** ([12 CFR 220.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/section-220.2)). Since T+1 took effect on 2024-05-28, that is **three business days**, not "pay by T+1 or it is a free-ride." Selling before the T+1 settlement date is not automatically freeriding if you pay with other funds on time. **Lab:** `examples/p1/11_freeride_txse_15c33_toy.py` sections A–C (weekdays only, no holidays).
- **Rule 15c3-3: keeping customer assets separate (checked 2026-11-11):** SIPC (next bullet) is a backstop if cash or securities go missing when a member firm fails. [Rule 15c3-3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-240.15c3-3) is a different rule, meant to reduce the chance those assets go missing in the first place. A broker that carries customer cash and securities must promptly obtain and then keep **possession or control** of customers' fully paid securities and excess margin securities (15c3-3(b)(1)). "Fully paid securities" are generally the securities in a cash account, plus other fully paid securities; shares the customer has not finished paying for are excluded (15c3-3(a)(3)). "Excess margin securities" are margin-account securities worth more than **140%** of the customer's debit balances, identified as not needed as margin (15c3-3(a)(5)). The firm must also keep a **Special Reserve Bank Account for the Exclusive Benefit of Customers** at a bank, holding cash and/or qualified securities in an amount computed under Exhibit A (15c3-3(e)(1) and [15c3-3a](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-240.15c3-3a)). In plain language: customer net credits must sit in that reserve account, not be used as the broker's working capital. This does **not** protect you against a fall in market prices, and it is not SIPC. The lab's section E is a credits-minus-debits toy, not the full official formula.
- **SIPC: what is protected if a broker fails (checked 2026-11-10):** the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC) protects customers' cash and securities that go missing when a SIPC-member brokerage firm gets into financial trouble. The limit is $500,000, which includes a $250,000 limit for cash ([SIPC](https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/what-sipc-protects)). It does **not** protect against a fall in the value of your securities, against being sold worthless stock, or against losses from bad advice, and it is not the same as FDIC insurance on bank deposits. SIPC states that customers need not live in or be citizens of the United States; a non-US citizen's account at a member firm is treated the same way. Commodity futures (except in a special portfolio margining account), foreign-exchange trades and digital assets that are not "securities" under the law are outside its scope. This lesson recommends no broker; to check whether a firm is a SIPC member, use SIPC's own website.
- **NBBO / SIP / IEX:** the NBBO is the consolidated **best bid + best offer** across exchanges; the SIP is the processor that publishes consolidated data. A single venue (e.g. IEX) can show a wider spread than the NBBO.
- **Rule 611 (Order Protection Rule) status box:** on 2026-06-11 the SEC issued a **proposal** to rescind Rule 611 (S7-2026-20, Release 34-105655, comments due 2026-08-17). The same proposal would also rescind **Rule 610(e)** (locked and crossed quotations). As of 2026-11-12 it is still a proposal, and the eCFR still lists §242.611 and §242.610(e). **Do not write that either rule has been repealed** ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/06/s7-2026-20)).
- **Sessions:** NYSE core trading is 09:30–16:00 New York time (ET); early close 13:00 on 2026-11-27; closed 2026-07-03 (Independence Day observed) ([NYSE](https://www.nyse.com/markets/hours-calendars)). Pre/post-market hours vary by venue and broker: **do not assume a uniform 04:00 start**, or 24/5.
- **Texas Stock Exchange (TXSE) (checked 2026-11-11):** the SEC approved Texas Stock Exchange LLC as a national securities exchange on **2025-09-30** (Release [34-104146](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-10-02/pdf/2025-19314.pdf), File 10-249, published at 90 FR 47880 on 2025-10-02). ## Rule 602: a published quotation is generally firm, not a hint (checked 2026-11-12)

Do not rewrite the **NBBO / SIP / IEX** paragraph above. Rule 604 (customer-limit display) is already taught in `execution-slippage`; do not rewrite it here. This section adds the Quote Rule’s firm-quote obligation, so a student does not treat displayed size as either infinite liquidity or a non-binding advertisement.

[17 CFR 242.602(b)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.602): subject to (b)(3), each responsible broker or dealer “shall be obligated to execute any order to buy or sell a subject security, **other than an odd-lot order**,” presented to it by another broker or dealer, or any other person belonging to a category of persons with whom such responsible broker or dealer customarily deals, “at a price at least as favorable to such buyer or seller as the responsible broker’s or dealer’s published bid or published offer … in any amount up to its published quotation size.” 602(b)(1): promptly communicate best bids, best offers, and quotation sizes. 602(b)(3) relieves the size or price obligation if, among other listed cases, a revised quotation size or a revised bid or offer was communicated before the order is presented, or the dealer is in the process of effecting a transaction and immediately afterward communicates the revision. 602(a)(3) unusual-market relief may apply under Commission-approved exchange rules: an exchange may notify specified persons and thereby relieve itself and its members of specified collection and firm-quote obligations while those conditions last.

Do not write that every SIP quote is a named dealer’s 602 firm quote in all cases, or that the customer can always “hit” the full NBBO size. Do not apply the firm-quote obligation to odd-lot orders. Do not ignore the (b)(3) revision exceptions or the (a)(3) unusual-market relief. Do not build an options 30-second-response engine under 602(c). Do not conflate 602 with already-taught Rule 604 (display), Rule 611 (trade-through), or Rule 605/606. No venue or broker recommendation.

## FINRA 5220: a member may not offer at a stated price unless prepared to buy or sell at that price and under the stated conditions (checked 2026-11-12)

Do not rewrite the already-taught Rule 602 paragraph above, the 600(b)(93) round-lot box, 610(e), 611, or 6380A. This section adds official FINRA 5220 so a student does not treat 602 as the only firm-quote rule, invent a 100-share “normal unit,” or treat every published quote as backing-away-proof. No product, broker, or venue recommendation.

[FINRA Rule 5220](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5220): “No member shall make an offer to buy from or sell to any person any security at a stated price unless such member is prepared to purchase or sell, as the case may be, at such price and under such conditions as are stated at the time of such offer to buy or sell.” Official Supplementary Material .01: under normal circumstances, a member making a “firm trading market” in any security “is expected at least to buy or sell a normal unit of trading in the quoted stock at its then prevailing quotations unless clearly designated as not firm or firm for less than a normal unit of trading when supplied by the member.” If, when an order is presented, the member is in the process of effecting a transaction in that security and immediately after completion communicates a revised quotation size, the member “shall not be obligated to purchase or sell the quoted security in an amount greater than such revised quotation size.” For this Rule, a “normal unit of trading” for an NMS stock “shall mean the “round lot” assigned to such NMS stock pursuant to Rule 600(b) of SEC Regulation NMS,” and “inter-dealer quotation system” is as defined in Rule 6420. Name Rule 6420 only; do not build that engine. Do not invent a 100-share normal unit; point at already-taught 600(b) round lots. Amended by SR-FINRA-2025-015, effective Dec. 3, 2025. This is a different firmness rule from already-taught Rule 602.

## T+1 is the Rule 15c6-1 default, not a law of every trade (checked 2026-11-12)

The T+1 sentence and the Regulation T payment-period paragraph above already teach that most US securities trades settle one business day after the trade date, and that the payment period is the settlement cycle plus two business days. Do not rewrite those paragraphs. This section adds the official settlement-cycle and delivery-date facts so a cleaner does not assume every tape print settles T+1.

[17 CFR 240.15c6-1(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-240.15c6-1): except as provided in paragraphs (b), (c), and (d), a broker or dealer “shall not effect or enter into a contract for the purchase or sale of a security (other than an exempted security, a government security, a municipal security, commercial paper, bankers' acceptances, or commercial bills) that provides for payment of funds and delivery of securities **later than the first business day after the date of the contract unless otherwise expressly agreed to by the parties at the time of the transaction**.” Paragraph (b) excludes unlisted limited-partnership interests, security-based swaps, and Commission-exempted contracts. Paragraph (c): contracts “for the sale for cash of securities that are **priced after 4:30 p.m. Eastern Time (ET)** on the date such securities are priced and that are sold by an issuer to an underwriter pursuant to a **firm commitment underwritten offering**” (or sold to an initial purchaser by a participating broker-dealer) are not under (a); the dealer shall not enter a contract for those securities “later than the **second business day** after the date of the contract unless otherwise expressly agreed … at the time of the transaction.” Paragraph (d) deems an alternate date agreed when the managing underwriter and the issuer have agreed to that date for all securities sold in such an offering and the parties have not expressly agreed to another date.

[FINRA Rule 11320](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/11320) “Dates of Delivery,” amended by SR-FINRA-2023-017 eff. May 28, 2024, catalogs the official types: **(a) cash** — delivery “on the day of the transaction”; **(b) regular way** — delivery “on, but not before, the first business day following the date of the transaction”; **(c) seller’s option**; **(d) buyer’s option**; **(f) delayed-delivery** — delivery “on the date agreed upon at the time for the transaction”; **(e)** contracts due on a day other than a business day “shall mature on the next business day.” Current 11320 has no separate “next day” category; do not invent one. 11320(g)–(h) delivery time-and-place rules exist; this lesson names that they exist and does not build a Uniform Practice Code or close-out engine.

Do not write that every trade must settle T+1, or that a cash contract settles T+1. Do not write that every IPO or follow-on settles T+2 — paragraph (c) is a listed after-4:30 p.m. firm-commitment branch, not a universal IPO clock. A not-regular-way contract can be a listed Rule 611(b)(2) trade-through exception (already taught); that does not make every such print a data error. This lesson makes no product or venue recommendation.

## Intermarket sweep orders (ISO): a current-law Rule 611 exception, not a repeal (checked 2026-11-12)

The status box above already says that the SEC proposal to rescind Rule 611 (S7-2026-20) was still a **proposal** as of 2026-11-12, and that eCFR still lists §242.611. Under current law, trading centers must still have policies reasonably designed to prevent trade-throughs of protected quotations in NMS stocks, **unless** an exception applies.

Two of those exceptions are the execution of an order **identified as an intermarket sweep order** (ISO) ([611(b)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.611)), and a trade by a trading center that **simultaneously routes** ISOs against the full displayed size of any better protected quotation that was traded through (611(b)(6)).

Under [17 CFR 242.600(b)(47)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.600), an ISO is a limit order for an NMS stock that meets both of the following: (i) when routed to a trading center, the limit order is identified as an ISO; and (ii) simultaneously with the routing of that order, one or more additional limit orders, as necessary, are routed to execute against the full displayed size of any protected bid (if selling) or protected offer (if buying) priced **better than that order's limit**, and those additional orders must also be marked ISO. The trading center, broker, or dealer responsible for the routing must take reasonable steps to establish that the order meets that definition (611(c)).

Tagging an order “ISO” without routing those simultaneous additional orders does not meet the definition. A print through a protected quotation can be lawful if a valid exception applies; it is not proof that Rule 611 has been repealed. This lesson does not teach routing recipes or recommend a venue.

**A print through a protected quotation can be lawful under a listed Rule 611(b) exception; ISO is only two of those exceptions (checked 2026-11-12).** ISO as 611(b)(5) and (b)(6), and the S7-2026-20 proposal status box, are already taught above. Do not rewrite the ISO definition or that status box: the proposal is still a proposal, and Rule 611 has not been repealed. Teach the rest of the official catalog so a cleaner does not treat every trade-through as a data error or as proof that Rule 611 has been repealed.

[17 CFR 242.611(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.611) exceptions include: **(2)** the transaction “was not a ‘regular way’ contract”; **(3)** the transaction “was a **single-priced opening, reopening, or closing transaction** by the trading center”; **(5)** and **(6)** ISO (already taught); **(7)** the execution of an order “at a price that was **not based, directly or indirectly, on the quoted price** of the NMS stock at the time of execution and for which the **material terms were not reasonably determinable** at the time the commitment to execute the order was made” (teach this rule text, not a product name). Other listed exceptions also exist and should be named as existing, not built into an engine: **(1)** the trading center displaying the protected quotation was experiencing a failure, material delay, or malfunction; **(4)** a protected bid was priced higher than a protected offer; **(8)** a flickering-quote branch (a best bid or offer equal or inferior to the trade-through price displayed within one second prior); **(9)** a customer stopped order meeting the rule’s three conditions.

Do not treat the official open, official close, or a halt-reopening print as a trade-through data error — they are the (b)(3) class. Reopening clocks after an LULD Trading Pause are taught in `market-regimes-crisis`. Do not invent exceptions that are not in 611(b). Do not build a compliance engine or teach routing recipes. Do not write that Rule 611 has been repealed.

## Rule 610(e): locking or crossing a protected quotation is still restricted (checked 2026-11-12)

The status box above already says that S7-2026-20 is still a **proposal** to rescind Rule 611, and that the same file would also rescind **Rule 610(e)**. Neither rule has been repealed.

Current [17 CFR 242.610(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.610) requires each national securities exchange and national securities association to have written rules that require members reasonably to avoid “displaying quotations that lock or cross any **protected** quotation in an NMS stock,” that are reasonably designed to assure reconciliation of locked or crossed quotations, and that prohibit a “**pattern or practice**” of displaying such quotations except as those rules permit. This is current law. Do not write that locked or crossed displays of protected quotations are already legal.

Keep 610(e) separate from **Rule 610(c)** access-fee caps, whose November 2027 compliance date is already taught above. Odd-lot and BOLO quotes are **not** protected (the next section), so a BOLO that locks or crosses the NBBO is not a regulatory lock or cross under 610(e). This lesson does not teach routing recipes or recommend a venue.

## Overnight trading and version traps (checked 2026-11-08)

**1. Overnight trading: planned, not “already market-wide 24/5”.**

- **ATS overnight sessions already exist:** some brokers offer overnight trading 20:00–04:00 ET through an alternative trading system (ATS). For example, Alpaca's doc says its overnight session runs 8:00 PM–4:00 AM ET via BOATS (an ATS) and accepts limit orders only ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/245-trading-for-trading-api)). Whether you can trade depends on the broker and the stock.
- **Exchange overnight sessions: planned for 2026-12-06, dependent on systems changes.** SEC 34-106042 says overnight protections are “expected to commence on December 6, 2026”, subject to systems changes by the SIP Processors ([SEC 34-106042](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-106042.pdf)); the CTA FAQ also gives 2026-12-06, “dependent on Listing Exchange readiness and regulatory approvals”, with a test-symbol-only Pre-Launch from 2026-11-08 to 12-05 ([CTA FAQ](https://www.ctaplan.com/publicdocs/ctaplan/CTA_Extended_Trading_Hours_FAQ.pdf)).
- **Trade-date assignment:** the CTA FAQ treats 21:00 to 20:00 the next day as **one trading day**; trades 21:00–24:00 count under the **next calendar date**.
- **Shanghai time:** on 2026-12-06 the US is on standard time (EST, UTC−5), so 21:00–04:00 ET = 10:00–17:00 Beijing time.

**2. Overnight price bands (LULD Plan Amendment No. 27, Phase 1).** Overnight Protected Hours run Sunday–Thursday 21:00 ET to 04:00 ET the next day. The Primary Listing Exchange computes two reference prices: the official Closing Price, and the consolidated last sale at 19:30 ET (Amendment No. 29 moved the time from 19:45 to 19:30; it is in effect on a **temporary** basis **not to exceed February 10, 2027** ([91 FR 65016](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20799/joint-industry-plan-notice-of-filing-and-order-of-summary-effectiveness-on-a-temporary-basis-not-to)), not a permanent Plan change unless and until the Commission issues a Rule 608(b)(2) approval; comments were due 2026-11-03 and have closed; the Commission will still issue a Rule 608(b)(2) order after considering comments; the operative date will be announced and is anticipated 2026-12-06, subject to SIP systems changes). The lower band is 20% below the lower reference price and the upper band is 20% above the higher one, with a minimum distance ($3 if the Closing Price is $1 or more, $1 if it is below $1). There is **no automatic Trading Pause**; the Primary Listing Exchange may declare a Regulatory Halt. This is only Phase 1 of two; this lesson says nothing about Phase 2. Details are in `market-regimes-crisis`.

**3. Version trap: Rule 612 ($0.005 tick) and Rule 610(c) (access-fee caps).** Like Rule 611 above, “written in a rule ≠ in force today”:

- The 2024 adopting release (34-101070) set a `0.005` dollar minimum increment for quotes and orders priced at $1 or more in **certain** NMS stocks, and cut the access-fee caps to `0.001` dollars per share (price $1 or more) and `0.1%` of the quote (below $1).
- Scope in eCFR §242.612: only stocks whose time-weighted average quoted spread over the evaluation period is `TWAQS <= 0.015` dollars get `0.005`; the others stay at `0.01`; quotes below $1 use `0.0001` ([eCFR §242.612](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/subject-group-ECFRac68bdd026a46db/section-242.612)).
- **Compliance date pushed back:** SEC 34-105656 (2026-06-11) extended the compliance dates for Rules 612, 610(c) and 600(b)(89)(i)(F) to the **first business day of November 2027** ([SEC 34-105656](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-105656.pdf)). So as of 2026-11-08, **do not** assume in a backtest that every stock already quotes in half-cents.

## Closing auction and round lots (checked 2026-11-10)

**1. Where the daily "close" comes from.** On a normal day the listing exchange runs a **closing auction** at 4:00 p.m. ET, and its price becomes the **official closing price**. Nasdaq calls it the NOCP (Nasdaq Official Closing Price, [Nasdaq Closing Cross FAQ](https://nasdaqtrader.com/content/productsservices/Trading/ClosingCrossfaq.pdf)). It is not always equal to the last trade printed on the consolidated tape, and trades after 4:00 p.m. do not change it. Different data vendors may define a daily `close` differently, so write down in your data note which close you use.

**2. The backtest trap: signal from the close, filled at the same close.** If you compute a signal from today's closing price and assume you also bought at that same closing price, you used information you could not have had when the order had to be sent. That is look-ahead. For NYSE-listed stocks, the NYSE auction page says that after **3:50 p.m. ET** Market-on-Close / Limit-on-Close (MOC/LOC) orders are only accepted if they offset the published imbalance, and cancel requests are rejected ([NYSE Auctions](https://www.nyse.com/trade/auctions)). So an honest "fill at the close" backtest needs a signal that is ready **before 3:50 p.m. ET**, or it should fill at the next tradable time instead. Other exchanges and other NYSE markets use different schedules; this lesson only uses the NYSE-listed time.

- In Beijing time, 3:50 p.m. ET is 03:50 the next day during US daylight time and 04:50 the next day during US standard time.
- Advanced note: auction imbalance feeds are often paid exchange data, so do not assume they are free features.

**3. "One lot" is no longer always 100 shares.** Under 17 CFR 242.600(b)(93), in effect since **2025-11-03**, the round lot depends on the stock's average closing price on its primary listing exchange during the prior evaluation period ([eCFR §242.600](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-242.600); [SEC 90 FR 51420](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-19926)):

| Average closing price | Round lot |
|---|---|
| $250.00 or less | 100 shares |
| $250.01 to $1,000.00 | 40 shares |
| $1,000.01 to $10,000.00 | 10 shares |
| $10,000.01 or more | 1 share |

- The tier is reset on the first business day of May (using March) and of November (using September). Newly listed stocks start at 100 shares. The latest reset was 2026-11-02; this lesson names no specific stocks.
- Why it matters for data: today's SIP NBBO is built from round-lot quotes; odd lots do not change it. (Regulation NMS also has rules for future competing consolidators, such as aggregating odd lots up to a round lot and rounding displayed sizes down to whole round lots; those apply to competing consolidators and self-aggregators, not to today's SIP NBBO.) When a high-priced stock's lot shrinks, quote sizes before and after the change are not directly comparable. Treat the change date as a data-version break.

**4. Odd-lot quotes (BOLO).** Since **2026-04-27** the SIP also publishes each exchange's best odd-lot bid and offer and a consolidated Best Odd Lot Order (BOLO). Odd-lot quotes are **not protected** under Regulation NMS and do not change the NBBO. Rule 610(e) speaks to **protected** quotations, so a BOLO that locks or crosses the NBBO is not a regulatory lock or cross ([UTP SIP Odd Lot FAQ v1.2](https://www.utpplan.com/DOC/Nasdaq%20UTP%20SIP%20Odd%20Lot%20Quotes%20FAQ%20Finalized%20Version%201.2.pdf)). Odd-lot **depth** information is exempted until the first business day of **May 2028** ([SEC 34-104612](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-104612.pdf)). Keep this separate from the Rule 612 half-cent tick above: that one is a different rule with a November 2027 compliance date.

## Opening auction: where the daily "open" comes from (checked 2026-11-11)

**1. The open is an auction price, and it may print after 9:30.** Each listing exchange runs an **opening auction**. On NYSE, orders can be entered from 6:30 a.m. ET, imbalance information is published from 8:00 a.m., and the opening auction process begins at 9:30 a.m.; the NYSE page adds that orders "can be entered and cancelled until the security is opened by the DMM, which may occur after 9:30 a.m." ([NYSE Auctions](https://www.nyse.com/trade/auctions); the DMM is NYSE's designated market maker for that stock). On Nasdaq, the Opening Cross sets the **Nasdaq Official Opening Price (NOOP)**; if a stock has no cross, the NOOP is the first last-sale eligible trade at or after 9:30 a.m. ([Nasdaq Crosses FAQ](https://nasdaqtrader.com/content/ProductsServices/Trading/Crosses/openclose_faqs.pdf)). So a daily `open` is not always "a trade at 09:30:00". As with the close, write down which open your vendor uses.

**2. The backtest trap is the same as at the close.** "Signal from today's open, filled at today's open" uses a price you could not know when the order had to be in. On Nasdaq, market-on-open (MOO) orders entered after the 9:28 a.m. ET cutoff are not accepted (same FAQ). So an honest "fill at the open" needs a signal ready from data available **before 9:28 a.m. ET** (Nasdaq-listed), or it should fill at a later tradable time. Other exchanges use different on-open cutoffs; this lesson only uses the two times above.

- In Beijing time, 9:28 a.m. ET is 21:28 the same day during US daylight time and 22:28 during US standard time.

## SIP sale conditions M, Q, and T: official open/close flags are not a regular last sale (checked 2026-11-12)

The previous sections already taught that the **official close** is the listing-exchange closing-auction price, which is not always the last tape print, and that the **official open** is an auction price that may print after 9:30. The SIP flags those events — and extended-hours prints — with sale-condition codes. This lesson teaches only a short official subset from the CTA CTS matrix, not the full catalog and not a vendor glossary.

In the [CTS Pillar Output Specification (ETH)](https://www.ctaplan.com/publicdocs/ctaplan/CTS_Pillar_Output_Specification_ETH.pdf) sale-condition matrix:

| Code | Name | Consolidated last | Consolidated high/low | Consolidated open | Participant last | Participant high/low | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M** | Market Center Official Close | No | No | No | Yes | Yes | No |
| **Q** | Market Center Official Open | No | No | Yes | No | Yes | No |
| **T** | Extended Hours Trade / Form T | No | No | No | No | No | Yes |
| **6** | Market Center Closing Trade | Yes | Yes | (CTS note #4; not the point here) | Yes | Yes | Yes |

Read that slowly. **M** is a market center's official close. It does **not** update the consolidated last, high/low, or open, and it does **not** update volume. It **does** update that participant's last and high/low. So an M print is not the *consolidated* official close, and folding M into a regular-hours last sale uses the wrong number. UTP Note 3 says the same idea in fewer words: M “shall only affect the market center closing/last sale value and will not affect the consolidated market value.”

**Q** is a market center's official open. It does **not** update the consolidated last or high/low, and it does **not** update volume. It **does** update the consolidated **open** and the participant high/low, but **not** the participant last. This lesson follows the CTS matrix for Q.

**T** (Extended Hours Trade / Form T) does **not** update consolidated or participant last, high/low, or open. It **does** update volume. It is **not last-sale eligible**. Prefer the [CTA Extended Trading Hours FAQ](https://www.ctaplan.com/publicdocs/ctaplan/CTA_Extended_Trading_Hours_FAQ.pdf), Version 1.2, last updated September 29, 2026, for the overnight clock. Q11: trades executed outside Regular Trading Hours (9:30 AM – 4:00 PM) “will continue to use the 'T' sale condition and are not Last Sale eligible. This applies to both the Extended Early Hours (9:00 PM – 9:30 AM) and any trades after the 4:00 PM close.” In 24-hour notation that early-hours window is **21:00–09:30** ET. Q12: ETH trades “are included in consolidated volume totals as per the Sale Conditions eligibility.”

**Version trap:** the UTP Data Feed Services Specification (September 2026) Note 1 still lists Form T windows as 12:00 a.m.–9:30 a.m. and 4:00:01 p.m.–8:00 p.m. That note has **not** been rewritten for 23/5. Do **not** use Note 1 to deny that overnight ETH prints are T. Prefer the ETH FAQ for the overnight clock, and treat the UTP note as stale on the window.

**6** (Market Center Closing Trade; UTP also labels it Closing Prints) **does** update the consolidated last, high/low, and volume. Do not treat every close-related flag as M.

**Backtest pitfall:** a series that folds T or M into the regular-hours last sale, or that uses an M print as the consolidated official close, is using the wrong number.

## FINRA 6380A: current off-exchange last-sale reports are due in 10 seconds, but not every tape print is already on that clock (checked 2026-11-12)

Do not rewrite the SIP sale-condition M / Q / T paragraphs above, or the “Effective TBD” 23/5 SIP-note sentences. This section adds the official off-exchange reporting clock, so a cleaner does not treat every tape timestamp as the execution time, or assume every overnight print is already on a 10-second clock.

[FINRA Rule 6380A(a)(1)](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/6380A): Trade Reporting Facility Participants shall, “as soon as practicable but no later than 10 seconds after execution,” transmit to the FINRA/Nasdaq Trade Reporting Facility last sale reports of transactions in designated securities “executed during normal market hours.” “Transactions not reported within 10 seconds after execution shall be designated as late.” The selected current rulebook version is “Mar 30, 2026 - Dec 05, 2026” (amended by SR-FINRA-2026-005 and SR-FINRA-2025-011, effective March 30, 2026). 6380A(a)(2): 4:00 a.m.–9:30 a.m. and 4:00 p.m.–8:00 p.m. ET are also “as soon as practicable but no later than 10 seconds,” with the outside-hours modifier. Transactions “executed between midnight and 4:00 a.m. Eastern Time shall be reported by 4:15 a.m. Eastern Time on trade date” — **not** on the 10-second clock. Transactions “between 8:00 p.m. and midnight,” or “on any non-business day (i.e., weekend or holiday),” shall be reported “the following business day by 4:15 a.m. Eastern Time” and designated “as/of.” 6380A(a)(3): execution time “shall be reported … in hours, minutes and seconds based on Eastern Time.” 6380A(e)(1)(C): transactions “reported on or through an exchange” shall not be reported to the FINRA/Nasdaq TRF. Supplementary Material .02: members must commence the trade reporting process without delay upon execution; “In no event may a member purposely withhold trade reports, e.g., by programming its systems to delay reporting until the last permissible second.” Supplementary Material .05 is a narrow temporary exception for specified qualifying overnight transactions, in effect until the earlier of a further TRF-hours amendment’s effective date or December 31, 2027; this lesson names that the exception exists and does not build an exception engine.

**Not yet effective:** SR-FINRA-2026-015 / SEC Release 34-105922 / 91 FR 45299 (July 20, 2026) was filed for immediate effectiveness, but “The implementation date of the proposed rule change will be the implementation date of the SIP Amendment.” The SIP Operating Committees anticipate December 6, 2026; FINRA intends to align, including if that date moves later. After implementation, trades during the new system hours (9:00 p.m. Sunday–8:00 p.m. Friday, pause 8:00–9:00 p.m. Monday–Thursday) would be due in 10 seconds, and trades executed while the TRF is closed would be due no later than 15 minutes after the TRF opens — **do not teach those future clocks as current law.** The rulebook also lists a “Dec 06, 2026 onwards” version; that is not the selected current text. Official May 22 and July 10, 2026 technical notices say FINRA’s ADF hours are not changing at this time. 6380B is the parallel FINRA/NYSE TRF rule; this lesson is not a 6380B / ADF / ORF catalog.

Do not apply 6380A to exchange prints. Do not write that every tape print is delayed by 10 seconds, or that “as soon as practicable” means one may wait until the tenth second on purpose. Do not treat midnight–4:00 a.m., or 8:00 p.m.–midnight, prints as already subject to the 10-second clock. Do not treat December 6, 2026 as an unconditional hard switch. Do not invent a 10b-18 23/5 mapping. No venue recommendation.

## Early-close MOC cutoffs, and Nasdaq vs NYSE close cutoffs (checked 2026-11-11)

**1. On an early-close day, NYSE's 3:50 p.m. cutoff moves earlier.** The 3:50 p.m. ET time taught above is for a regular day that closes at 4:00 p.m. NYSE Rule 7.35(a)(8) defines the Closing Auction Imbalance Freeze Time as **10 minutes before the scheduled end of Core Trading Hours**. A 2026 NYSE regulatory memo states that clock times such as 3:50 p.m. apply on days whose scheduled close is 4:00 p.m.; "On days when the NYSE is scheduled to close at a time other than 4 p.m., such deadlines—tied to the scheduled end of Core Trading Hours—move accordingly" ([NYSE RM 3.20.2026 n.2](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/markets/nyse/rule-interpretations/2026/Q1_2026_Quarterly_Expiration_RM_3.20.2026.pdf)). So when the close is 1:00 p.m. ET, the free MOC/LOC entry cutoff is **12:50 p.m. ET**. In 2026 the NYSE cash equity markets close early at 1:00 p.m. ET on **2026-11-27** (the day after Thanksgiving) and **2026-12-24** ([ICE / NYSE holiday announcement](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2025/NYSE-Group-Announces-2026-2027-and-2028-Holiday-and-Early-Closings-Calendar/default.aspx); [NYSE hours](https://www.nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars)). Both days fall in US standard time, so 12:50 p.m. ET is **01:50 the next day** in Beijing time. An honest "fill at the close" on an early-close day needs a signal that is ready before 12:50 p.m., not the usual 3:50 p.m.

**2. On a regular day, Nasdaq's on-close cutoffs are later than NYSE's.** For NYSE-listed stocks, after 3:50 p.m. ET only offsetting MOC/LOC orders are accepted. For Nasdaq, the Closing Cross FAQ says that MOC/LOC orders may still be entered after 3:50 p.m. ET but may not be canceled or modified; **Nasdaq stops accepting new MOC orders at 3:55 p.m. ET**; LOC orders may be entered until **3:58 p.m. ET** ([Nasdaq Closing Cross FAQ](https://nasdaqtrader.com/content/productsservices/Trading/ClosingCrossfaq.pdf)). Do not copy the NYSE 3:50 p.m. cutoff onto a Nasdaq-listed close-fill. That FAQ states those clock times against a 4:00 p.m. close; it does **not** give Nasdaq's early-close schedule, and this lesson does not invent one.

## Tokenized stock venues (TSV): a separate data source (checked 2026-11-10)

Since 2026-09-17 an SEC exemptive order ([Release 34-106402](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-106402.pdf)) lets a **Tokenized Securities Venue (TSV)**, an on-chain venue that trades tokens representing US-listed stocks through an **AMM** (automated market maker: a pool that prices trades by formula, not an order book), operate under conditions without being treated as an exchange. Its legal status is in `compliance-ethics`; here only the data point matters.

- **A TSV print is not an exchange print.** The order says a TSV that meets the conditions is not an exchange and is not subject to Regulation NMS: because it is exempt from the definition of "exchange", it is not a trading center or market center under Regulation NMS. That conclusion is about the TSV itself. The order gives broker-dealers that participate in a TSV no Regulation NMS relief; section VI, question 7 of the order asks for comment on whether it should. Its trades are not exchange trades, and its pool prices are not exchange quotes.
- **The price is set differently.** AMM pools generally price from the ratio of the assets in the pool, and the order itself warns that token prices could dislocate from the stock's price in traditional format.
- **The data format is its own.** The TSV must publish transaction data in US dollars, machine-readable, for the past 30 days, updated within 10 minutes of each trade. Transaction time should be in UTC, and "daily" volume covers the 24 hours before the TSV's publication time, not the 09:30–16:00 ET session.
- **Halts carry over.** The TSV must stop trading a token when the primary listing exchange stops trading the underlying stock.

**Backtest rule:** keep TSV data in its own table with a `venue` / `source` column; do not merge it into exchange daily bars or use it to fill gaps; convert UTC to ET before comparing it with exchange data; and do not use a TSV price as the fill price for a strategy that trades the listed stock. Volume is capped by the order, so a TSV print also says little about how much you could trade.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Treating mid as the market-buy fill.
2. Same-bar signal×return while claiming no look-ahead.
3. Pretending the spread is “not a cost.”
4. Treating toy bps as a real exchange schedule.
5. Asking for / designing HFT recipes (out of scope).
6. Treating T+1 as “24 hours” or settling a Friday trade on Saturday; calling the Rule 611 or Rule 610(e) rescission proposal “repealed”; assuming pre-market always starts at 04:00.
7. Writing exchange overnight trading as “the US is already market-wide 24/5”, or assuming every broker and every stock can trade overnight; treating overnight price bands as limit-up/limit-down or a fill guarantee; assuming the $0.005 tick already applies to every stock.
8. Treating the daily close as the last trade without checking the vendor's definition; computing a signal from the 4:00 p.m. close and filling at that same close; assuming one lot is always 100 shares, or that a better odd-lot quote moves the NBBO.
9. Reading SIPC as protection against market losses, or as the same thing as FDIC deposit insurance.
10. Merging TSV (tokenized-stock venue) trades into exchange bars, or reading a TSV print as an exchange print or NBBO quote; comparing UTC TSV times with ET exchange times without converting.
11. Computing a signal from today's official open and filling at that same open; assuming every daily `open` is a trade at exactly 09:30:00.
12. Using 3:50 p.m. ET as the NYSE MOC cutoff on an early-close day; copying the NYSE 3:50 p.m. cutoff onto a Nasdaq-listed close-fill; or inventing Nasdaq early-close clock times the official FAQ does not state.
13. Treating a sale before T+1 settlement as automatically freeriding, or saying the Regulation T payment period is T+1 rather than the settlement cycle plus two business days; treating Rule 15c3-3 as the same thing as SIPC, or as protection against a fall in prices; assuming historical SIP data already includes TXSE prints under code F, or giving TXSE the same 8:00 p.m. ET post-market hours as some other venues.
14. Tagging an order “ISO” without simultaneously routing the additional limit orders against better protected quotations, and still calling it an ISO; or reading a print through a protected quote as proof that Rule 611 has been repealed.
15. Backtesting a stop-loss as a guaranteed fill at the stop price; treating a gap, a halt, or a wick as a fill at the stop; assuming every broker triggers off last sale.
16. Writing that locked or crossed displays of protected quotations are already legal, or that Rule 610(e) has been repealed; treating a BOLO/NBBO lock as a regulatory lock or cross under 610(e); conflating 610(e) with the Rule 610(c) access-fee caps.
17. Folding a T or M print into the regular-hours last sale, or using an M print as the *consolidated* official close; treating every close-related flag as M; using the stale UTP Note 1 Form T windows to deny that overnight ETH prints are T.
18. Treating the official open, official close, or a halt-reopening print as a trade-through data error; inventing exceptions that are not in 611(b); reading a trade-through as proof that Rule 611 has been repealed.
19. Writing that every trade must settle T+1, or that a cash contract settles T+1; inventing a current-law “next day” delivery category; writing that every IPO or follow-on settles T+2.
20. Writing that every SIP quote is a named dealer’s 602 firm quote in all cases, or that the customer can always hit the full NBBO size; applying the firm-quote obligation to odd-lot orders; ignoring the (b)(3) revision exceptions or the (a)(3) unusual-market relief; conflating 602 with Rule 604, 611, or 605/606.
21. Applying 6380A to exchange prints; writing that every tape print is delayed by 10 seconds, or that “as soon as practicable” means one may wait until the tenth second on purpose; treating midnight–4:00 a.m. or 8:00 p.m.–midnight prints as already on the 10-second clock; treating December 6, 2026 as an unconditional hard switch, or inventing a 10b-18 23/5 mapping.
22. Treating FINRA 5220 as already-taught Rule 602; inventing a 100-share “normal unit of trading” instead of the official Rule 600(b) round lot; omitting the official “not firm / firm for less than a normal unit” designation or the in-process revised-size clause; or building a Rule 6420 engine.
