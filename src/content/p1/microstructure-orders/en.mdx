## Learning goals

This lesson (~**7 hours**: 3h concepts + 3h exercises + 1h quiz) builds microstructure intuition: spread, **mid**, market/limit, slippage; why same-bar zero-cost fills are over-optimistic. Prereq: `markets-language`.

**Goals:** hand-calc mid and market-buy bps; write a fill/cost sheet; compare close vs next-open (and shift) narratives.

**Must-teach:** mid=(bid+ask)/2; market buys lean ask; impact/latency intuition only — **no HFT recipes**.

## mid and market-buy (hand calc)

Given bid=99.90, ask=100.10:

- mid = (99.90+100.10)/2 = **100.0**
- teaching market-buy fill = ask = **100.10**
- cost vs mid = (100.10/100 − 1)×10000 = **10 bps** (half-spread)

<Formula label="Mid">
mid = (bid + ask) / 2
</Formula>

## Half-spread on the CSV

On `synthetic_ohlc_ba.csv` (n=252): mean half-spread ≈ **6.22 bps**, median ≈ **6.36 bps**.

## Fill timing: same-bar look-ahead vs honest

Parameters: signal = close > MA5.

| Narrative | Meaning | Lesson NAV |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| optimistic same-bar | today’s close in signal **and** today’s close return (**LOOK-AHEAD**) | ≈ **2.39** |
| honest shift(1) | trade next period’s return | ≈ **1.04** |
| honest next-open | fill next open, earn open→close | ≈ **1.04** |

Same-bar zero-cost fills inflate curves — ban them explicitly in backtests.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Treating mid as the market-buy fill.
2. Same-bar signal×return while claiming no look-ahead.
3. Pretending the spread is “not a cost.”
4. Treating toy bps as a real exchange schedule.
5. Asking for / designing HFT recipes (out of scope).
