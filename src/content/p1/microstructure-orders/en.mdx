## mid and market-buy (hand calc)

Given bid=99.90, ask=100.10:

- mid = (99.90+100.10)/2 = **100.0**
- teaching market-buy fill = ask = **100.10**
- cost vs mid = (100.10/100 − 1)×10000 = **10 bps** (half-spread)

<Formula label="Mid">
mid = (bid + ask) / 2
</Formula>

## Fill timing: same-bar look-ahead vs honest

Parameters: signal = close > MA5.

| Narrative | Meaning | Lesson NAV |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| optimistic same-bar | today’s close in signal **and** today’s close return (**LOOK-AHEAD**) | ≈ **2.39** |
| honest shift(1) | trade next period’s return | ≈ **1.04** |
| honest next-open | fill next open, earn open→close | ≈ **1.04** |

**Why are `nav_honest_shift1` and `nav_honest_next_open` equal here?** The CSV generator set `open[t] = close[t-1]` (script prints `open_equals_prev_close_all_rows_after_first True`). Then next-open→close returns match the close-to-close path under a `shift(1)` position. Real markets often gap overnight, so the two NAVs can diverge — this equality is a synthetic simplification, not a general theorem.

Same-bar zero-cost fills inflate curves — ban them explicitly in backtests.

## US main line: settlement, accounts, quotes and sessions (checked 2026-11-08)

**Default scope:** US-listed stocks, USD. This is mechanism literacy, not broker rules or investment advice.

- **T+1:** since 2024-05-28 most US securities trades settle **one business day** after the trade date ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-62)). It is not “24 hours”, and it does not mean you cannot sell the same day. Weekends and exchange holidays are not business days.
- **Cash vs margin account:** a cash account buys with settled funds; a margin account borrows from the broker and has maintenance margin. The PDT successor rule and its phase-in are in `compliance-ethics` (not repeated here).
- **NBBO / SIP / IEX:** the NBBO is the consolidated **best bid + best offer** across exchanges; the SIP is the processor that publishes consolidated data. A single venue (e.g. IEX) can show a wider spread than the NBBO.
- **Rule 611 (Order Protection Rule) status box:** on 2026-06-11 the SEC issued a **proposal** to rescind Rule 611 (S7-2026-20, Release 34-105655, comments due 2026-08-17); as of 2026-11-08 it is still a proposal and the eCFR still lists §242.611. **Do not write “repealed”** ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/06/s7-2026-20)).
- **Sessions:** NYSE core trading is 09:30–16:00 New York time (ET); early close 13:00 on 2026-11-27; closed 2026-07-03 (Independence Day observed) ([NYSE](https://www.nyse.com/markets/hours-calendars)). Pre/post-market hours vary by venue and broker: **do not assume a uniform 04:00 start**, or 24/5.
- **Best execution:** FINRA 21-23 notes Rule 5310 best execution is **not solely about price** ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/21-23)).

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Treating mid as the market-buy fill.
2. Same-bar signal×return while claiming no look-ahead.
3. Pretending the spread is “not a cost.”
4. Treating toy bps as a real exchange schedule.
5. Asking for / designing HFT recipes (out of scope).
6. Treating T+1 as “24 hours” or settling a Friday trade on Saturday; calling the Rule 611 rescission proposal “repealed”; assuming pre-market always starts at 04:00.
