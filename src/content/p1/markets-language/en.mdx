## Intuition: the research chain

A defensible research statement usually is:

1. **Hypothesis** (falsifiable) → 2. **Signal** (clear information time) → 3. **Risk/constraints** → 4. **Attribution** (vs which benchmark, costs deducted?).

A programmer’s edge is often turning that chain into a **versioned, reproducible** pipeline—not a one-off clever formula.

## Alpha and Beta (numeric example)

**Beta:** co-movement with the benchmark.  
**Alpha:** residual mean after subtracting Beta×benchmark (teaching annualization often ×252).

The lab uses a known DGP: `true_alpha_daily=0.0001`, `true_beta=1.2`, `n=251`, `seed=11`, `Rf=0`. Estimates: `beta_hat≈1.22`, `alpha_hat_ann≈0.084` (sample noise ≠ exact truth).

<Formula label="Teaching OLS">
beta = cov(r, r_m) / var(r_m);  alpha_d = mean(r) − beta * mean(r_m)
</Formula>

**Look-ahead risk:** OLS uses aligned contemporaneous returns; do not sneak `shift(-1)` prices into the signal before talking about Alpha.

## Friction checklist

At least five (the script prints six): bid-ask spread, commissions, slippage/impact, borrow/financing, delayed fills, taxes (venue-dependent). Gross-only stories that ignore frictions are dishonest — the last P1 lesson quantifies this.

## Three-sentence research goal (template)

The script fills a reproducible template: **market/sample → constraints & annualization → success criteria (net Sharpe/drawdown, frozen OOS)**. Goals must be testable, not merely “sound smart.”

## CSV→returns→NAV

Data: `examples/p1/data/synthetic_ohlc_ba.csv` (generation seed 11, n=252).

```python
raw = pd.read_csv(CSV, parse_dates=["date"]).set_index("date")
ret = raw["close"].pct_change()
nav = (1 + ret.fillna(0)).cumprod()  # day-1 fill 0 only for a teaching NAV path
```

Parameters: `Rf_daily=0`. Final NAV ≈ **1.106**.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Targeting high absolute return without constraints or drawdown.  
2. Calling Beta exposure “Alpha.”  
3. Claiming live-ready without a friction list.  
4. Non-reproducible research (no seed/CSV/version).  
5. Defining today’s signal with future returns (lookahead).
