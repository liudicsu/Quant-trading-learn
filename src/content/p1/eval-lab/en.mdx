## Learning goals

This lesson (~**5 hours**: 1.5h concepts + 3h lab + 0.5h quiz) builds a **net-of-cost** metric table: toy MA signal vs buy-and-hold; slippage bumps; why win rate ≠ a good strategy. Prereqs: `returns-vol-corr`, `microstructure-orders`.

**Goals:** build a net-of-cost table; explain win rate ≠ good strategy.

**Must-teach:** gross-only reporting is dishonest; turnover amplifies costs.

<Callout type="warn" title="Not investment advice">
Toy signal — not a trade recommendation.
</Callout>

## Parameters

- n≈251; MA window **10**; `position = signal.shift(1)` (anti look-ahead)
- Rf=0; ann √252
- One-way cost levels: **0 / 5 / 15 bps**
- Win-rate trap demo: seed=3, n=1000

## Net-of-cost table (highlights)

Buy-and-hold gross Sharpe≈0.66; toy MA net Sharpe≈0.39 at 0 bps, ≈0.19 at 5 bps, and **turns negative at 15 bps**. Annualized one-way turns ≈ **49** — costs scale with turnover.

## Win-rate trap

Seed=3 toy path: win rate≈**0.91** but **negative** mean return. High win rate can hide rare large losses.

## Slippage bump experiment

Same signal, higher one-way bps → worse net NAV/Sharpe. Always report the cost assumption beside the metrics.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Posting gross curves only.
2. Using win rate instead of expectancy and drawdown.
3. Ignoring turnover×cost linkage.
4. No benchmark comparison.
5. Evaluating “net of cost” without shifting the signal (still mixed with look-ahead).
