## 1. Spot

**Definition:** You hold the underlying (or a broker book entry). P&L roughly follows price; usually cash or stock margin — no futures-style expiry.

**Numeric example:** Buy 10 shares at 50, exit 51 → P&L = 10×(51−50) = **+10** (`spot_long_pnl 10.0`).

## 2. Futures

**Definition:** Standardized forward contracts with expiry, margin, and mark-to-market; common for hedging and price discovery — more machinery than “buy a share.”

**Numeric example:** 1 contract, multiplier 50, entry 4000 → exit 4010 (long) → P&L = 50×(4010−4000) = **+500** (`futures_long_pnl 500.0`).

## 3. ETFs

**Definition:** Fund shares on a basket; often used as a **tradable benchmark** proxy; still subject to premiums/discounts and tracking error.

**Numeric example:** Buy 20 shares at 100, exit 99 → P&L = 20×(99−100) = **−20** (`etf_long_pnl -20.0`).

## Long vs short P&L (±2%)

Teaching unit: 1 unit at price 100.

| Move | Long P&L | Short P&L |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| +2% (→102) | +2 | −2 |
| −2% (→98) | −2 | +2 |

Shorts can profit when prices fall in this toy — real borrowing, margin, and squeeze risk still apply.

## Margin and liquidation (numeric)

Toy: notional 100,000; **initial margin** 10% = **10,000**; **maintenance** 7% = **7,000**.  
Equity ≈ initial margin + P&L; a breach of maintenance is the teaching trigger.

Critical return: (7000 − 10000) / 100000 = **−3%**. If entry is 4000, illustrative liquidation price ≈ **3880**.

Linear mark-to-market toy only — **not** any exchange’s real rulebook.

## Formulas

Single-period simple return r_t.  
**Compound NAV** from 1:

<Formula label="Compound NAV">
NAV_T = ∏_(t=1)^T (1 + r_t)
</Formula>

**Arithmetic cumulation (simple-style sketch):** 1 + sum r_t — for teaching contrast.  
**Leverage toy** (no financing): product of (1 + L * r_t).  
**Tracking error (annualized sketch):** std(r_port − r_bench) * sqrt(252).

## Worked numeric example

Two periods +10%, −10%:

- Compound: 1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99 (lose 1%)
- Calling the average return “0” hides the path.

## Benchmark and tracking error

A good research benchmark is **investable, reproducible, and pre-specified**. The script builds 63 synthetic days of benchmark vs portfolio and prints:

- `tracking_error_daily ≈ 0.001739`
- `tracking_error_ann ≈ 0.0276` (~2.8% annualized)

Lower TE means tighter tracking; low TE alone is not a “good strategy.”

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Treating futures/ETFs as “tiny stocks” while ignoring expiry, premiums, and TE.
2. Discussing shorts without frictions and squeeze risk.
3. Advertising compound products with simple arithmetic (or the reverse).
4. Picking the benchmark after seeing results (data snooping).
5. Exporting teaching leverage/liquidation toys as position advice.
6. Reporting mean excess without tracking error.
