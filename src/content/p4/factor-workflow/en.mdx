## Parameters

- Panel: **200 assets × 750** business days; **10 industries**; log market caps
- DGP: `r = beta_true * f_true + beta_mkt * f_mkt + beta_size * f_size + industry + noise`
- **Strong (inflated teaching):** `MU_STRONG=0.0025`. **Weak (realistic foil):** `MU_WEAK=0.0010`
- Winsorize p=1%; CS z-score; neutralize via residual on industry dummies + **`log_mcap.shift(1)`** (prior-day mcap only); re-z
- Costs: P2 stack — **10 bps** one-way + √impact; impact σ = **lagged** rolling std (VOL_LB=20, `shift(1)`, expanding warm-up)
- Look-ahead: factor at `t` predicts returns from `t+1`; portfolio weights enter via `shift(1)`

## Factor construction

Winsorize, z-score, then **residualize** on industry and **lagged** log market cap so the cleaned factor recovers the planted loading.

Lab recovery (strong; mean CS corr vs `beta_true`): raw ≈ **0.71** → neutralized ≈ **0.92**. Neutralization uses `log_mcap.shift(1)` so the code matches the no-lookahead text.

## Strong vs weak (must-teach)

Live single factors are usually **weak**. The strong panel is inflated on purpose; the weak panel is the honesty check.

| Panel | MU | RankIC | ICIR | gross SR | net SR | mean turn |
|-------|-----|--------|------|----------|--------|-----------|
| strong (inflated) | 0.0025 | ≈0.112 | ≈4.64 | ≈4.50 | ≈3.67 | ≈1.06 |
| weak (realistic foil) | 0.0010 | ≈0.043 | ≈1.77 | ≈1.62 | ≈0.79 | ≈1.06 |

Same turnover, much smaller RankIC/ICIR on weak — **costs eat a larger fraction of the edge**. That is why researchers combine many weak, imperfectly correlated factors rather than trusting one loud backtest.

## IC, RankIC, ICIR, decay

**RankIC** = Spearman correlation between factor[`t`] and forward returns. **ICIR** ≈ mean(IC)/std(IC)·√(252/h).

Strong lab: RankIC h=1 mean ≈ **0.112**, ICIR_daily ≈ **4.64**. Cumulative horizons raise mean RankIC for a **static risk loading**; a **decaying-alpha foil** falls from ≈0.60 (h=1) to ≈0.04 (h=20).

**Overlapping windows:** for h>1, overlapping forward sums mainly inflate **IC autocorrelation** and shrink the naive ICIR standard error — they do **not** chiefly bias mean IC. Fix with **non-overlapping** IC sampling or **Newey–West** on the IC series.

![IC decay](/p4/ic_decay.png)

## Fama–MacBeth and FF3/FF5

**Fama & MacBeth (1973, JPE 81(3), 607–636):** period-by-period CS slopes, then time-series mean/t (optional Newey–West).

**Fama & French (1993, JFE 33(1), 3–56):** FF3 = Mkt−Rf, SMB, HML. **Fama & French (2015, JFE 116(1), 1–22):** FF5 = FF3 + RMW + CMA.

Strong FM: daily t_iid ≈ **7.82**, monthly ≈ **6.37**. The weak panel often would **not** clear HLZ’s t>3 — another reason the strong MU is labeled inflated.

## Factor zoo and Harvey–Liu–Zhu (2016)

**Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016, RFS 29(1), 5–68)** argue new factors should clear roughly **t > 3** after the published zoo’s multiple testing.

Lab: **200** random CS factors on the strong panel.

| Threshold | Observed | Null expectation (N(0,1) two-sided) |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------------------|
| \|t\|>2 | ≈6.0% (12 of 200) | ≈**4.55%** (≈**9.1** of 200) |
| \|t\|>3 | ≈0.0% (0 of 200) | ≈**0.27%** (≈**0.54** of 200) |

The observed ~4.5–6% rate at \|t\|>2 is **right in line with pure noise**. That is why HLZ raise the bar toward **t>3**: the industry already spent the t≈2 budget.



## Cost-aware factor ranking (Detzel–Novy-Marx–Velikov)

[Model Comparison with Transaction Costs](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13225) (Detzel, Novy-Marx & Velikov, *Journal of Finance* 2023) shows that **ignoring trading costs biases tests toward high-turnover factors / models**. Gross Sharpe crowns can reverse once costs enter.

Lab toy (no look-ahead; same lagged information, different turnover):

```text
slow_factor  turnover≈0.506  gross_SR≈-0.119  net_SR@10bps≈-1.929
fast_factor  turnover≈3.197  gross_SR≈0.363  net_SR@10bps≈-11.368
ranking_by_gross_picks=fast  ranking_by_net_picks=slow
```

**Teach:** cost-blind gross rankings bias toward high-turnover signals; net-of-cost rankings can reverse. Paper: after costs, FF5 (especially cash-profitability variants) beats high-cost q / six-factor style comparisons on their design.



## Costs × turnover taxonomy (Novy-Marx & Velikov RFS 2016)

Next to Detzel’s *model comparison with costs*: [A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721) (NBER w20721; published *RFS* 2016 29(1) 104–147, [DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhv063](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv063)).

**Spirit (not a homework alpha list):**

- **Gross zoo ≠ net zoo** — transaction costs shrink profitability and significance; data-snooping concerns rise.
- Anomalies with **one-sided monthly turnover ≲ 50%** mostly keep statistically significant *net* spreads when designed with cost mitigation (e.g. buy/hold spreads).
- **Few** high-turnover strategies survive net.
- Capacity of new capital to erode profits is **inversely** related to turnover (size/value/profitability-style books tend to have more capacity) — literacy only.

Lab buckets reuse the Detzel foil (slow vs fast turnover → net survival flips).

**Name-drop — FIM capacity:** Frazzini–Israel–Moskowitz *Trading Costs* (SSRN 3229719) is often cited beside this taxonomy for **capacity ↔ turnover** spirit. **No capacity-as-alpha table** in this course.

<Callout type="warn" title="Debunk">
Do not treat taxonomy rows as a shopping list of alphas. Costs kill many gross “winners.”
</Callout>


## Crowding and decay

Factor rank autocorr lag-1 ≈ **0.88**: sticky ranks lower theoretical turnover but raise crowding if books converge.

### Bridge — crowding → LLM homogenization

When many desks chase the same value/momentum/size expressions, capacity and decay worsen even if in-sample IC looked fine. LLM factor miners face the **same** risk in a new wrapper: without originality / complexity / hypothesis-alignment constraints they **homogenize** toward crowded classics ([AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16789); lab foil in `frontier-llm-agents`). Teach: report trial count N, prefer sealed OOS + costs/DSR, and treat “another RSI clone” as crowding — whether a human or an agent wrote the formula.

## Look-ahead warnings

- Never use same-bar returns in the factor you trade that bar.
- Neutralization mcap must be **`log_mcap.shift(1)`** (known at t).
- Impact σ lagged; forward returns aligned from t+1.
- Overlapping multi-day IC → prefer non-overlap or Newey–West for ICIR inference.

## Harvey–Sancetta–Zhao (NBER w34898, 2026): t ≥ 3 and local FDR

Next to HLZ (2016) and Detzel costs: [What Threshold Should be Applied to Tests of Factor Models?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34898) (Harvey, Sancetta & Zhao) argue proper inference needs dependence across tests, a correct null, and selection-bias mitigation. Their framework yields a **lower bound**: use a **t-statistic cutoff of at least 3.0**. Prefer the **NBER URL** (DOI pages may 500).

They also advocate the **local False Discovery Rate** — roughly the probability the null is true **given** the realized test statistic. A classical **p-value is not** P(null | data).

Lab sketch:

```text
noise_t≈1.2: two-sided_p≈0.2301  localFDR_sketch≈0.957
borderline_t≈2.0: two-sided_p≈0.0455  localFDR_sketch≈0.668
HLZ_bar_t≈3.0: two-sided_p≈0.0027  localFDR_sketch≈0.091
planted_t=4.2: two-sided_p≈0.0000  localFDR_sketch≈0.003
```

Statistical clearance still needs **economic** significance after costs (Detzel bridge).

## Admission gates pointer + Hou–Xue–Zhang replication failure rates

**Pointer:** Host detail for **admission gates / injected-truth** lives in `backtest-traps` ([arXiv:2610.07701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07701)). Takeaway for factor research: gates beat random-admission mainly for weak train **t ≲ 2.5**; adoption often ~1–7%; always score on **excess**, not absolute. **门槛 ≠ 提高收益.**

**Hou–Xue–Zhang (2020 RFS) replication foil** — next to HLZ + Harvey–Sancetta–Zhao: [Replicating Anomalies](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhy131) shows most published anomalies **fail** under a common replication protocol.

```
paper: 65% of 452 anomalies fail |t|≥1.96 (NYSE breakpoints + value-weight)
       82% fail multiple-testing |t|≥2.78
lab toy mixture: fail_|t|<1.96≈87%  fail_|t|<2.78≈97%  (order-of-magnitude foil)
```

Even “successful” replications are often economically smaller than the original reports. Treat the failure rate as a **prior** — new factors still need HLZ/HSZ hurdles **and** Detzel costs, not a blog t-stat.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

- Treating HXZ failure rates as a license to skip HLZ/HSZ + Detzel — they are a prior, not a free pass.
- Using absolute-return admission gates on factor panels.

1. Skipping neutralization then calling industry exposure "alpha."
2. Treating the inflated strong panel as a live RankIC benchmark.
3. Celebrating gross curves before the shared cost model (esp. on weak signals).
4. Treating t>2 as a discovery after mining dozens of factors — compare to the ~4.55% null.
5. Using same-day mcap in neutralization (lookahead).
6. Reading ICIR from overlapping windows without NW / non-overlap.
7. Not dividing Ken French percents by 100; subtracting RF from `Mkt-RF` again; treating -99.99 as a real return.
