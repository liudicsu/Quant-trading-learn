## Learning goals

This lesson (~**14 hours**: 5h concepts + 7h labs + 2h write-up — HEAVY) builds a repeatable **cross-sectional factor research** workflow: construction, IC/RankIC/ICIR/decay, quantile long–short with costs, Fama–MacBeth, and a Harvey–Liu–Zhu multiple-testing foil. Prereqs: `strategy-compare-lab`, `backtest-traps`.

**Goals:** implement Spearman IC; build quantile long–short; contrast true vs null factors.

**Must-teach:** IC = cross-sectional rank agreement; RankIC / ICIR / IC decay; winsorize + z-score + industry/mcap neutralization; quantile L/S + turnover/costs; Fama–MacBeth (+ FF3/FF5 literature); factor mirage & Harvey–Liu–Zhu t>3; start from an economic hypothesis; Alphalens/Qlib-style workflow in plain pandas.

## Parameters

- Panel: **200 assets × 750** business days; **10 industries**; log market caps
- DGP: `r = beta_true f_true + beta_mkt f_mkt + beta_size f_size + industry + noise`
- `f_true` mean = **0.0025**/day (teaching-scale premium); raw factor = `beta_true` + 0.4·industry + 0.25·size + noise
- Winsorize p=1%; CS z-score; neutralize via residual on industry dummies + log mcap; re-z
- Costs: P2 stack — **10 bps** one-way + √impact; impact σ = **lagged** rolling std (VOL_LB=20, `shift(1)`, expanding warm-up)
- Look-ahead: factor at `t` predicts returns from `t+1`; portfolio weights enter via `shift(1)`

## Factor construction

Winsorize extreme cross-sectional tails, standardize to z-scores, then **residualize** on industry and log market cap so the cleaned factor recovers the planted loading instead of industry/size contamination.

Lab recovery (mean CS corr vs `beta_true`): raw ≈ **0.71** → z-only ≈ **0.71** → neutralized ≈ **0.92**. Neutralization is the step that lifts recovery.

## IC, RankIC, ICIR, decay

**RankIC** = Spearman correlation between `factor[t]` and forward returns. **ICIR** ≈ mean(IC)/std(IC)·√(252/h).

Lab (neutralized): RankIC h=1 mean ≈ **0.111**, ICIR_daily ≈ **4.65**. Cumulative horizons raise mean RankIC for a **static risk loading**; a separate **decaying-alpha foil** falls from ≈0.60 (h=1) to ≈0.04 (h=20). Both are IC-horizon lessons — do not confuse them.

## Fama–MacBeth and FF3/FF5

**Fama & MacBeth (1973, *JPE* 81(3), 607–636):** each period, cross-sectionally regress returns on loadings; then take the time-series mean of the slope and its t-stat (optionally Newey–West HAC).

**Fama & French (1993, *JFE* 33(1), 3–56):** FF3 = Mkt−Rf, SMB, HML. **Fama & French (2015, *JFE* 116(1), 1–22):** FF5 = FF3 + RMW + CMA.

Lab FM on the planted factor: daily mean γ ≈ **0.00212**, t_iid ≈ **7.77**, t_NW(L5) ≈ **7.31**; monthly t_iid ≈ **7.89**. Citations verified against RePEc / journal records.

## Factor zoo and Harvey–Liu–Zhu (2016)

**Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016, *RFS* 29(1), 5–68), “…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns”:** with extensive multiple testing across the published factor zoo, new factors should clear roughly **t > 3**, not the conventional t≈2.

Lab: **200** random CS factors → frac |`t`|>2 ≈ **0.045**, frac |`t`|>3 ≈ **0.000**; planted true factor FM t ≈ **7.8**. A t≈2 “discovery” among many trials is not enough.

## Crowding and decay

Factor rank autocorr lag-1 ≈ **0.87**: sticky ranks lower theoretical turnover but raise **crowding** risk if many books converge. Pair with IC-horizon charts before claiming a multi-week hold.

## Expected-value framing

Planted premium makes IC/FM pass on purpose so you can **audit recovery**. Live research starts from an economic hypothesis, reports trials N, and treats t≈2 under a zoo as weak. Net of costs and turnover decides tradeability — Gross SR alone is not a product.

## Look-ahead warnings

- Never use same-bar returns in the factor that you trade that bar.
- Neutralization covariates must be known at `t` (industry, lagged mcap).
- Impact σ must be **lagged** (no full-sample std).
- Forward-return alignment: `factor[t]` ↔ returns[`t+1 ... t+h`].

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Skipping neutralization then calling industry exposure “alpha.”
2. Celebrating gross quantile curves before the shared cost model.
3. Treating t>2 as a discovery after mining dozens of factors (Harvey et al. 2016).
4. Confusing rising cumulative RankIC (static risk loading) with a decaying trading signal.
5. Full-sample volatility inside impact costs (look-ahead).
6. Same-bar fills or forward returns aligned on `t` instead of `t+1`.
