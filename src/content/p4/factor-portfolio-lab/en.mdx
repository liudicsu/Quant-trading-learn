## Learning goals

This lesson (~**7 hours**: 2h concepts + 4.5h lab + 0.5h quiz) turns a cleaned factor into a **portfolio experiment**: neutralization before/after, single vs equal-weight multi-factor honesty, turnover–cost schedules, and crowding. Prereq: `factor-workflow`.

**Goals:** estimate factor-portfolio turnover; sketch size/industry neutralization impact; compare single vs EW multi-factor.

**Must-teach:** combining factors does not auto-raise tradeability; turnover and the shared cost model decide net returns; before/after neutralization contrast; crowding and decay.

<Callout type="info" title="Learning only — not investment advice">
Same planted 200×750 panel. The nuisance `raw_factor2` has **no** premium — blending it should hurt, not help.
</Callout>

## Parameters

- Reuse P4-01 panel + cached neutralized true factor; second raw factor = noise loading + industry tint
- Quintile L/S; hold schedules 1/5/10/20 days; costs = P2 10 bps + lagged √impact
- Look-ahead: `shift(1)` weights; `factor[t]` → return[`t+1`]

## Neutralization before / after

Lab RankIC mean: before ≈ **0.085** → after ≈ **0.111**. Nuisance factor after neut ≈ **0.003** (~0). Time-series mean of neutralized factor recovers `beta_true` at corr ≈ **0.97**.

## Single vs EW multi-factor

| Spec | net SR | mean turn | RankIC |
|------|--------|-----------|--------|
| true_only | ≈3.73 | ≈0.93 | ≈0.111 |
| nuisance_only | ≈−7.11 | ≈1.56 | ≈0.003 |
| ew_true+nuisance | ≈3.09 | ≈1.32 | ≈0.081 |

Blending a null factor **dilutes** IC and net SR while raising turnover. Combining ≠ free lunch.

![NAV compare](/p4/portfolio_nav_compare.png)

## Turnover–cost table

Holding the same quintile weights for 5–20 days cuts mean turnover from ≈0.93 to ≈0.05 and lifts net SR on **this** sticky planted signal. Expected-value tradeoff: freshness vs cost drag. Live signals with fast IC decay may prefer shorter holds.

## Crowding foil

Long-leg HHI mean ≈ **0.025** (equal weight in ~40 names) with rank AC1 ≈ **0.87**. Sticky, overlapping books across funds are the crowding channel — even when your backtest looks clean in isolation.

## Expected-value framing

Gross SR of a planted factor is not a product. Net of the shared cost model, plus an honest null-factor blend test, is the minimum bar before any “multi-factor upgrade” claim.

## Look-ahead warnings

- Do not neutralize with future mcap or next-day industry assignments.
- Freeze/hold logic must not peek at future ranks inside the hold window.
- Impact σ lagged only.

## Common mistakes / pitfalls

1. Equal-weighting every candidate factor without a null control.
2. Ignoring turnover when declaring a multi-factor “improvement.”
3. Neutralizing with future information.
4. Assuming high rank AC always means low crowding (it can mean everyone holds the same book).
5. Reporting only gross NAVs.
